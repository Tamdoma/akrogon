# Pitfalls for anyone, not only beginners

## What was not good
Version 3 framed the per-question warning and the map's third part as "what a beginner would miss". That reads as a note for novices and lets a practitioner skip it. The traps that cost the most in August were practitioner traps: a driver parsing prose, a second watcher, parking under a new name.

## What the updated way does
Version 4 renames the line to "Pitfalls anticipated" and widens its intent: the traps behind this choice for anyone, from the practitioner evidence and the agent's own reasoning. The map's third part is now pitfalls anyone could fall into.

## Why
A pitfall is a property of the choice, not of the reader. Naming it for everyone keeps the operator and the agent both looking.

## How it gets improved
If a pitfalls line ever repeats the option text, it is not a pitfall, cut it. Track whether a named pitfall later shows up as a defect anyway, that is the signal the line is decorative.
