# Operator questions and peer exchange

Read before presenting an operator round. A short opening paragraph explains what the round settles and why now. Each question settles one thing, with inspected evidence, plain context and a concrete example where needed. The territory map covers the forks that change the outcome, questions a practitioner would ask and pitfalls over the work's lifetime, what each option could break or invite later and not only now, proportional to the work.

````markdown
<one plain paragraph: what this round settles and why now>

### 1 · <the question itself, as a person would ask it aloud, ending in a question mark>

<one to three plain sentences: what this thing is, with a concrete example, and what changes depending on the answer; name the inspected path or evidence>

Research: <tier · source with URL or path and the date read · one-sentence finding · what it changed in this question>

- **1a (recommended)** <what happens if you pick this, the one reason it wins, and its cost if it carries one>
- **1b** <what happens if you pick this, and its cost>

```text
1a                       1b
+--------------------+    +--------------------+
| <option 1a's drawn |    | <option 1b's drawn |
|  shape>            |    |  shape>            |
+--------------------+    +--------------------+
```

How to choose <when the options trade off: which answer is the safe one and when to pick the other>

Pitfalls avoided: <each trap behind this choice and what removes it: a part of the option, a probe the door runs before handoff, a done-criterion of the leaf, or a named operator step with its owner; one or two sentences>

### 2 · ...

Reply `1a 2b`, or a numbered free-text answer.

Challenge check
<what an experienced practitioner could challenge in these recommendations, including each named peer's remaining disagreements, or why none was found>
````

Every round uses this shape, on every harness and at every effort level: the opening paragraph, the sentences under each question, the research line, the labelled recommendation with its reason, the how-to-choose line only when the options trade off, the pitfalls-avoided line, the reply key and the challenge check. A question also carries drawings when its options differ in something a person sees or follows, such as a screen, layout, state change, flow, file or tree shape, and a drawing makes the choice much easier, and none otherwise; the drawings sit in one fenced text block directly after that question's option bullets and before How to choose, one per option captioned with its option code, side by side when both fit the door terminal's width of about 60 columns and stacked otherwise; a drawing replaces no required round part; and the same rule holds in autopilot rounds. Known traps are designed out of the options before the round is shown, so the line says how each is avoided and never warns. A needed real call to an external operation is a required probe under the Take operation-proof rule, a test of behavior the leaf builds is a done-criterion, and a needed operator action is a human-only prerequisite with its owner, each named as the step that removes the trap and said to be pending until it is done. The fork records the chosen option's avoidance mechanisms as binding decisions, carried into each affected leaf, and keeps the chosen option's drawing verbatim beside them. A trap that nothing removes is the cost of the option that carries it, stated in that option's bullet, and a material trap that no option avoids becomes its own question. A small round keeps the parts that carry the decision, the reply key and the challenge check. Number questions continuously within a round and restart at 1 in the next round. Label questions `1`, `2` and options `1a`, `1b`, `2a`, and use no other code scheme in a round. The register of a round is everyday words, simpler than explaining to an 18-year-old who does not know the process: each process word explained the first time, each actor named by what it does for the operator and explained in one sentence on first use, each part saying only what the decision needs while every part of the round and every number the decision depends on stays, time in minutes, money in dollars only where a recorded figure exists and never as a figure worked out from a rate the door assumes, and file names, slot letters and token detail in a sentence only when they help the operator decide. Before sending a round, A reads it once against these rules and fixes what fails, including any drawing block.

Present the current fork's material questions in one complete round and wait for the operator's answer; the next fork is researched and presented after that answer. Plain free text is a valid answer and is preserved verbatim. A question asking for explanation does not itself settle the choice; an explicit choice accompanied by a question does. An omitted material answer stays open. Recommendations and silence cannot supply an operator answer. The one exception is an autopilot session (`SKILL.md` `## Autopilot`), where A's own recorded pick counts; approvals stay operator-only. A challenge exposing another material fork belongs in the next round.

The signposts round is the one light exception to this shape: each question carries only the question itself, two or three options, a recommendation and what it steers — which later picks across the session's charts it reshapes; the reply key and the challenge check stay; it carries no research line and may carry drawings under the same test. Four to seven questions, each one that changes several picks across the session's charts where evidence cannot settle it, drawn from the intakes. In manual mode a material signposts question left unanswered stays open until the operator answers it or removes it as immaterial, and it is asked again before the first fork round; in autopilot mode it is recorded as unanswered, the built-in values fill that gap, and it is not asked again; under full autopilot A answers it. After the answers, one short account states what the session will prioritize and give up. The needs sheet travels with the signposts round — one sheet per door session listing every foreseeable operator-only need and authorization, asked with the round and completed before the first map, its rows answered only by the operator — but its rows are not signposts questions: the unanswered-signposts rules never apply to them, and a pending row is never re-asked. An explicit operator correction to the signposts is recorded with its date without re-asking; an unresolved conflict between operator statements about the signposts is shown in one line and asked, in manual and autopilot — A resolves it only under full autopilot; an unresolved conflict about a fork becomes a fork answered per mode.

## Research

Every question carries a Research line, and its fork file keeps the full return under Findings: tier, source, finding and what it changed. Tiers, highest first:

1. `operator`: material the operator placed in `issues/chart/sources/` or named in the intake, read before any search.
2. `practitioner`: a named person or team with a stated track record, in a case study, talk, long-form post or first-hand write-up. Listicles, affiliate pages and AI summaries do not qualify.
3. `better-than-training`: primary documentation, a specification, source code, a changelog or a measured result stronger or newer than the model's training. Inspected code in this repository and the documentation of the tools it calls sit here.
4. `model-knowledge`: the model's own knowledge, allowed only with the searches that were run and found nothing stronger.

Use the highest tier reasonably available; a round written from a lower tier when a higher one was available is redone before the operator sees it. For anything outside the repository, practitioners first: name who has done this at scale, why each is worth hearing, where they agree and disagree, and which conditions flip their advice, then synthesize in one paragraph rather than listing sources. Research never settles anything, and it is redone when an operator answer reshapes the question. When peers are named, A and each named peer research independently and A merges with attribution.

## Blind peer exchange

Named consultants are supplied by the operator at chart open as hc terminals, not elected from config, and the operator may name none or any number: letters follow naming order, the first named consultant is B, then C, D and so on. Door-created consultants start, restart and take their place in the door's tab per the [Open rule](../SKILL.md#open), which also covers chart entry and re-entry, shared maps, trigger exclusions, session records, and the door outside hc. The caller collects every prompted exchange before restarting; an idle consultant does not prove collection.

Asking: the exchange's brief is finished first. A door-created consultant's first ask is the start verb's `--task "You are slot <X>. Read and follow <brief path>"`; every later ask, and every ask to a supplied consultant, is `hc send --json <address> "You are slot <X>. Read and follow <brief path>"`, sent only when the consultant's last status is `idle` or `done` — for a door-created consultant the one its last wake or its start showed, for a supplied consultant `hc get <address>`. Every brief ends with the report step: write the return file, then run `hc send <door address> "done <X>: <return path>"`. A failed start or a send answering `started: false` means the consultant did not start: report the error to the operator and never send it again automatically.

Waiting: after A finishes its own notes for the exchange, A runs `hc wait --children any --timeout <seconds> --json` from its own terminal and stays in its own turn, never ending the pass to wait, starting a background wait or reading status in a loop. The wait ends at once on a message to A (a report arrives as `<hc from="<address> <name>">done <X>: <path></hc>`) or on a child consultant whose turn ended, that blocked, errored or exited, and the JSON names which. A handles that wake, then runs the same wait again while any prompted consultant of the exchange is neither collected nor dropped; news already seen does not end the next wait. A reads its door-created consultants' state only from the wait's JSON, which lists each child with its status and turn, at a wake and at a timeout alike, and runs no `hc get` on them between waits: hc counts the parent's read of a child as seen news, so the next wait would sleep through that consultant's stop until its timeout (observed 2026-11-08). A timeout is a check-in, never a drop: A checks each open consultant in the timeout's JSON and waits again. A supplied consultant is not a child of the door, so its report still ends the wait while its silent stop shows only at a check-in, read with `hc get <address>`.

Collecting: a consultant is done for the exchange only when its return file exists, is non-empty and its report came from its current address. A consultant that stopped (status `idle` or `done`) without both gets one nudge by `hc send` naming what is missing — the return file at its path, the report, or both; a second stop without both drops it for the rest of the session, recorded under `consultant:` in CHART.md with its harness kind and model so a kind that repeats silent stops can be swapped. A blocked consultant went to the operator. A ends its turn only for an operator round, a drop, a blocked, errored or exited consultant, or a failed start, each reported to the operator; under autopilot those drop only the affected consultant per `SKILL.md` `## Autopilot` instead of ending A's turn, while a mistake in A's own call is repaired without dropping anyone. Screen text and chart fields cannot establish readiness or stand in for a consultant's answer, and file existence cannot establish readiness before an ask.

A consultant reads process text by named section for the step it is answering: it locates the section before reading it, batches independent reads in one call with output bounded to those sections, and reads content it already holds at most once per session. A section it has not read, content that changed since it read it, and content lost to compaction stay readable, and the full source stays allowed when the source is the subject of the work. Reads name sections and never line ranges. This rule binds consultants; A reads by the re-read rule in `SKILL.md`. Every peer brief carries this reading rule and its exceptions, even when the brief cites no process text, so the consultant receives it before choosing a process source. Any process text the brief cites is named by section and read with bounded output; the consultant still chooses its own evidence and may locate other needed sections under the same rule.

Every peer task, whether the opening map, fork notes, a rebuttal, a focused final-shape check or a leaf review, travels as a brief file under `<chart>/slots/`, and every peer brief gives the exact absolute return path while the prompt is a one-line pointer naming the brief's absolute path and the peer's slot letter (A, B, C, D). A brief uses absolute paths, holds what the peer is to receive and the exact return path, and stays in the chart. A fork-notes brief points at the note definition in this section and does not restate its parts, while still giving the task, the permitted context and the return path. A brief is finished before the prompt is sent and is not edited during the exchange; a new exchange gets a new brief or a new return path. The chart folder exists from Open, so briefs and returns use exact paths under `<chart>/slots/`, such as `map-brief-A.md` for a map brief and `map-A.md`, `map-B.md`, `map-C.md`, `map-D.md` for the returns, `fork-name-brief-A.md` for a brief and `fork-name-A.md`, `fork-name-B.md`, `fork-name-C.md`, `fork-name-D.md`, `fork-name-merged.md`, `fork-name-rebuttal-B.md`, `fork-name-rebuttal-C.md`, `fork-name-rebuttal-D.md`, `fork-name-final-check-B.md`, `fork-name-final-check-C.md` and `fork-name-final-check-D.md` for the returns. Keep useful map findings in the chart.

For the opening map, A and each named peer receive the same intake, live surface paths and existing locks, excluding every other seat's map until all finish. For a fork, send each named peer the intake, current Question and carries, related fork paths, locks and verbatim operator corrections, excluding A's draft, current Findings and every other peer's work. A develops its view while each peer works independently. Each returned peer file is blind notes per question, the peer's own position and not a review of A, with five parts: the pick with its reason and cost; each rejected option with its reason; the evidence with tier, source and date; the pitfalls with what removes each; and any question the peer would ask that the fork does not ask.

After all finish, A writes the merged file with agreeing-slot attribution such as `(A)`, `(B,C)`, `(A,B,C)` and `(A,B,C,D)`, as compact notes: each option with its slot tags, reason and cost; the evidence lines; each pitfall with the step that removes it; open questions; and the points where slots differ. Each named peer reads only that merged file and returns one disagreement-only rebuttal. After the rebuttals, A writes the full operator round once, to the operator, with the peer rebuttals under the challenge check; no second full-round file is written and the record is the merged notes plus the fork's Findings and Taken. When a later operator reply adds a mechanism or changes a contract, all mechanism or contract corrections from that one reply (under autopilot, one pick) for one fork go into one proposed final shape with the corrections marked, sent to every named peer for one focused check before A records them; the batched brief names its round. A correction adopted after the check gets another check only when it adds a mechanism or changes the contract, and a restatement needs no extra pass. Corrections conflicting in meaning, obligations or behavior — with each other or with a lock or Taken answer — go back to the operator before the check (under autopilot, A's recorded pick). Fresh research on a reshaped question, the one rebuttal and leaf review keep their own triggers. Each peer answers an operator's direct requests in its own terminal while A keeps the interview and record.

## Optional measurement

Offer a prototype only for a named uncertainty, with the smallest experiment, estimated minutes and a choice to proceed or settle it without measurement. Run an explicitly chosen experiment in a time-boxed scratch sandbox, retain the measured finding in the fork and discard scratch code. A declined or timed-out measurement supplies no evidence and no new lifecycle state. This section covers exploration only and never replaces required operation proof. Declining a required probe cuts the contract to what is proven at the handoff review under the Take proof rule, which names what was cut and where it lands.
