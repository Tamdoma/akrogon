# Sanity check standard

Same standard `chart-issues` uses for research. Fetch the source before citing it. Record the source and the tier in the paragraph that holds the thought.

## Tiers

Tier 1: a named practitioner reporting first-hand, or a primary text by its author. Examples: an engineering post by the people who ran the system, a book by its author, a maintainer's own benchmark repo.

Tier 2: official documentation, a specification, or a measured result with its method visible. The operator's own measurements from the repo's git log or files count as tier 2 and need no outside source; name the command or the path instead.

Tier 3: model knowledge with no fetched source. Use only when nothing better exists, and say so in the sentence.

## Recording shape

Inside the operator's paragraph, in the operator's voice: "Sanity check, tier N, <who or what>, <where>: <one or two sentences of what it says, quoted where the wording matters>." Then how it lands on the thought: it holds, it holds with a correction, or it does not hold, and why in one sentence.

Corrections to a fact the operator stated go beside the fact, in the operator's voice, as "one correction I was given and accept" or "one correction I was given and do not accept, because". The operator decides which.

## What a check is not

Not a decision. Not a recommendation. Not a rewrite of the thought. If the check produces a solution, the solution goes to chat, and the file gets one sentence saying advice was received and the point is open.
