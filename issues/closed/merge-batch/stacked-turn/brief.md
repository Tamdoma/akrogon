# Brief: stacked-turn

## What
When a repo's merge turn starts and the holder rebases mechanically, the command stacks every other unstopped leaf already waiting in `merge` onto the holder, in merge-queue order, and the holder's B verifies that one stacked tree. The merge prompt names the stacked leaves. A solo turn (the holder cannot be rebased mechanically) stacks nobody. Green pushes the whole stack once and moves the holder and every stacked leaf to `merged`. Red on a stack with more than the holder moves no leaf to `check.fix`: B's usual red call (`akrogon phase <slug> check.fix --slot B --attempt <id>`) records the stacked heads as seen red on that base, clears the batch and starts the holder-alone turn at once. A leaf whose head was in a red stack is not stacked again on the same base, and is stacked again once its head or the remote default branch changes; it takes its own turn as holder in queue order either way. No cap on stack size, no split search, no culprit command, no batch-size setting.

## Why
Leaves wait in line for the merge turn. In framework since 2026-10-14 12:00, 38 merge visits took 9.0 h, median 13.7 min, while a lone verify takes 5.2-7.5 min; about 4.5 h was spent waiting for the turn (issues/chart/overnight-recs/slots/brief.md I4). The wait grows with the number of ready leaves. One green stacked verify lands several leaves for the cost of one. Commit 6640ab5 removed the old batching (members, split, --culprit, batch_limit, -2.9k lines) because it was too much machinery; this keeps only the all-or-holder part.

## Done-criteria
1. Two or more leaves in `merge` with changes that rebase cleanly: the holder's B gets one prompt naming the stacked leaves, one `akrogon verify` runs on the stacked tree, the existing `merged --check` trailer and retired-lesson checks cover every stacked leaf's commits, one fast-forward push lands them, and every stacked leaf reaches `merged` with its completion owner checked (`issue complete` / `epic complete` printed when it finishes one).
2. A waiting leaf whose rebase onto the stack conflicts is left out of that stack; the holder and the other stacked leaves still land, and the left-out leaf merges in a later turn. A holder that cannot be rebased mechanically takes a solo turn with nothing stacked. A stopped leaf is never stacked.
3. A red verify on a stack holding more than the holder: B's `check.fix --attempt <id>` call moves no leaf, records each stacked head and the stack's base in the existing attempt history, and itself starts the holder-alone turn without waiting for `next` or the watcher. A recorded head is not stacked again on the same base; after the remote default branch moves, or its head changes, the leaf is stacked again. A red on the holder alone ends as today.
4. A stacked leaf whose head changed, that left `merge`, or that was stopped while the stack was being verified is never pushed as part of that stack; the push is refused and the stack is rebuilt.
5. After a push that lands the stack, a crash before the phase moves leaves the record needed to finish them: the next akrogon run, proving the landing once by the stack's pushed candidate being an ancestor of the tracking ref, moves every stacked leaf still in `merge` to `merged` exactly once and skips those already moved.
6. `skills/merge-issue/SKILL.md` describes the stacked turn: the holder's B verifies the stacked tree, makes no in-place repair on a red stack of more than the holder, gathers the completion owner briefs of every stacked leaf, and the endings above. `src/AREA.md` and docs that describe the merge turn say the same.
7. No stack-size cap, split, culprit command or batch-size setting exists in `src/` or the config schema.
8. The repo's `test`, `typecheck` and `format` checks pass.
