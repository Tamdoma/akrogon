1. **F1 · Create fails on the documented draft and accepts reserved names.** The chart skill puts ISSUE.md in the owner directory, but `src/direct.ts:69` demands it inside the leaf and skips readiness.yaml. The real CLI refused the documented draft and accepted a draft with no readiness.yaml. It also accepted a new draft whose owner was already closed, and a slug already held by a nested epic leaf. The depth-two scan in `src/state.ts:229` misses the latter and create never checks the closed or parked owner path. This blocks ordinary handoffs, can make a leaf unreadable as a duplicate, and can defer a known owner collision until completion. Use one consistent layout across the command, chart skill, shapes and fixture, and cover the readiness and owner/slug refusals with the real CLI. Criterion 1, criterion 10 and design 2. Evidence in `implementation/check-review-b-direct-entrypoints-1795632878678-1318600.log` and `tests/direct-create.test.ts:32` (writes two ISSUE.md files and no readiness.yaml).

Add the refusal before allocation and prove it at the CLI. Criterion 3 and design 4. Evidence in `implementation/check-review-b-direct-seat-1795632906143-1329971.log`.
3. **F3 · Drop rejects charts with captures and moves records without capture or phase coordination.** The charting probe writes logs under `<chart>/direct/` (`skills/chart-issues/SKILL.md:47`). A normal abandon with that directory present returned 1, even though `<chart>/direct/<owner>` was free. `src/direct.ts:126` checks and replaces the parent instead of moving into the named owner child. The same verb checks phase before any shared phase lock, and its only lock covers the Git worktree removal. A concurrent review-to-merge can advance after the eligibility read, and a live capture can still be publishing when the owner moves. Use the destination and locking from design 3, including the enclosing capture check and shared owner lock. The existing fixture bakes in the wrong path and seeds the success in implement with no review/report/log preservation assertion. Prove the review-phase drop with chart captures present, the kept artifacts, and the phase/capture boundary. Criterion 9. Evidence in `implementation/check-review-b-direct-drop-status-1795632934110-1341688.log`, `tests/direct-drop.test.ts:213`, `src/phase.ts:401` and `src/phase.ts:987`.
4. **F4 · Direct broadcast re-sends after a late delivery and holds cleanup after the reviewer is gone.** An hc send that times out may still have reached B. Once that turn advances, lifecycle settlement records the original delivery (`src/next.ts:946`); the direct path calls send again (`src/next.ts:1524–1551`). This can produce a second broadcast. The test at `tests/direct-land.test.ts:501` exercises a definite pre-send refusal and requires a retry, leaving the ambiguous late-delivery case uncovered. After an acknowledged broadcast, cleanup also ignores the recorded turn and keeps the slice/temp/worktree on a missing B (`src/reconcile.ts:200`), so an operator closing B after the prompt can leave those resources held indefinitely. The cleanup test uses the repo's own hc core, leaving the required cross-session hold and release unproved. Cover late settlement and the cross-session turn/absent cases at the CLI. Criterion 8 and design 13.
5. **F5 · Pending direct broadcasts disappear from the text status table.** After the land moves the owner to closed, `src/status.ts:940` filters the row out of plain `akrogon status` during the wait for a working B. The DETAIL and JSON views still report pending. The fixture also returns JSON group `["..","closed","broadcast-chart"]` because `src/status.ts:624` computes the group's path from the open area for a closed owner. The operator's normal status view loses the pending NOTE, and JSON consumers get parent navigation segments in an owner group. Criteria 4 and 8. Evidence in `implementation/check-review-b-direct-drop-status-1795632934110-1341688.log`.
6. **F6 · The timing cohort never includes direct visits.** A direct leaf created by the door enters implement and records no planning prompt. `src/timing-report.ts:356` builds its entire cohort from delivered planning prompts, then applies the direct flag only after membership is decided. The direct cohort path is therefore unreachable for normal attempts, while `tests/direct-timing.test.ts:173` proves only the single-slug command. Include the direct attempt's start in the cohort and prove a direct visit is present without seat or prompt gaps. Criterion 5 and design 6.
7. **F7 · The installed workflow omits its review routing and broadcast wait.** The door can follow `skills/chart-issues/SKILL.md:121` through `Closed` and reuse or restart the reviewer while a broadcast is pending or running. The skill omits the wait and the rerun-to-repoint recovery in design 14. The branch's `skills/check-issue/SKILL.md:10` still says every initial review takes both slots and repair belongs to B, and does not instruct the direct reviewer to use the handed-off worktree/base from the consultant's normal repo-root cwd. `skills/implement-issue/SKILL.md` likewise says A and B are next after every implement. The command expects B alone and routes repair through A, so a reviewer following the installed instructions can use the wrong checkout or leave the door waiting for a second verdict or a B repair. Settle the direct instructions and document the wait before moving on. Criterion 10 and design 10, 11, 14 and 15.
8. **F8 · Rerun can overwrite a concurrent state change.** Repointing B while a land or listener pass operates on the same leaf is a normal recovery action. `src/direct.ts:62–65` probes outside the engine lock and `src/state.ts:253` reads and writes the whole state without that lock. If a listener writes `prompted.B` or a phase move saves state between the read and rename, the rerun can clobber the new fields, e.g. reopening a completed broadcast for another send. Serialize the read/modify/write with the same state lock as phase/dispatch, re-resolve the leaf under it, and preserve current non-seat fields. Criterion 2 and design 2/13.
