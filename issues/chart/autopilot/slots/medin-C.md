## Pick

Take four ideas, each landing in a named fork. Reject the factory architecture. Cost: one new Boundary question, heavier switch-on.

1. **Intent is its own hold class (Boundary, new question).** Medin, Shapiro and StrongDM keep the human at the spec inlet: epic, in/out scope, acceptance scenarios. Charting is that stage. Split questions by kind. How a mechanism satisfies stated intent: decide. What the product should want, unstated in the intake: hold. Wider than "fact only the operator has": a taste question can look like judgment and still be intent. Test: could the operator hate the result if every check passes? If yes and unanswered, hold.
2. **Switch-on carries the epic (Switch).** The toggle is the switch-on statement: named intake, destination, what is never touched, exceptions such as direct pre-authorized. No toggle machinery. Delegation text is the control surface and gives Boundary's bound something written to test against.
3. **No editing the grading surface (Boundary).** The summary's rule self-modification failure (agent deletes the blocking lint rule) sharpens the own-rules difference. Hold any leaf whose work weakens what grades it: chart-issues itself, `checks`/`merge_checks`, criteria, intake, off-route lines, the delegation record. No new immutable-context files: those existing records are it, and the door cannot rewrite them to make a chart pass.
4. **Measure satisfaction (Morning record).** StrongDM (verified) measures satisfaction as a fraction over many runs. Charting version: morning record counts picks accepted unchanged, corrected, or reversed per session. That rate, not one good night, is the evidence. Blind notes already give the holdout property that matters: consultants write without seeing A's pick.

Does not apply: the timer manager agent is the watcher (operator-run, off route), and a door-side timer hits the no-clocks lock. Splitter and cheap-model routing duplicate the door's split and slot config, and cheap triage contradicts the operator's model-floor answers. Sprawl maps weakly, as rule and criteria growth, covered by the picking values.

The challenge to autopilot itself (planning stays human) holds for intent forks, not mechanism forks. It survives only because the operator supplies the inlet at switch-on, and most later questions are mechanism choices with inspectable evidence. The one successful night decided mechanism questions (delete a scan, temp on disk), not intent. That sample is biased to the decidable class, which is why point 1 is needed.

## Rejected options

- Adopt the factory whole (splitter, router, manager, staging). Duplicates lifecycle and watcher, akrogon has no staging branch, timer manager is foreclosed. More machinery is this chart's own historical failure mode.
- Read "planning stays human" as no autopilot charting at all. The human already sits at the inlet, and the operator record (89% of 582) shows most questions after it are not intent questions.
- Secret holdout criteria hidden from implementers. Criteria sit in the brief by design. Hiding them breaks the contract for a problem blind notes and deliberate-break proofs already address.
- A per-step HITL toggle system. Switch-on text carries the same information, no new state.

## Questions the chart does not ask
