## I4: implement the small batch design now, without promising fixed gains

Pick: reuse the one merge turn. At turn acquisition, snapshot the holder plus all currently ready compatible followers. Do not wait for a batch to fill. Stack the holder first, then each follower's original change against its recorded base. Skip a conflicting follower after restoring the last good temporary tip. Green verifies and pushes the complete stack once. A red combined run records the member heads, rebuilds the original holder alone and verifies that alone. No split search, culprit command, caps, parallel trains or new locks.
A head seen in a red batch is ineligible only as a follower until it changes. It remains eligible as the normal queue holder, otherwise innocent unchanged leaves can starve forever. A canceled/incomplete run is not proof of a red batch. Do not perform mixed-stack repairs before fallback, since those changes have no clear leaf owner.

Changes: `src/phase.ts:514,557` validates all members' proof/trailer obligations, accepts only the full tested candidate and completes all landed members. `src/next.ts:924` must reconcile every member after a crash following push, not only the holder. `src/attempts.ts:28,60` records member heads in the existing attempt history for failed-follower exclusion. Estimate: roughly 200–400 production lines plus 300–600 focused test lines, including member persistence, fallback and crash recovery. This is a design estimate, not a measured patch. Reuse queue ordering, receipts, fast-forward push and reconciliation rather than restore the removed 2.9k-line system. The simple stacking loop is a small fraction of the work.
What can break: duplicate patches from stacked branches, member heads changing while the holder runs, false member completion after crash recovery, dirty worktrees, and stale receipts after main advances. Preserve original member ranges, compare captured heads before push, and make completion replay idempotent against the recorded landed candidate. Test red fallback, conflicting followers, unchanged-head solo eligibility, push-then-crash recovery and non-fast-forward rebuilding. Do not mistake a rebased commit SHA mismatch for an unlanded member.
Quality: combined verification proves the final main tree, not each intermediate member prefix. Cross-leaf changes can mask defects. Keep each leaf's independent criterion proof and review, and make this distinction explicit rather than promise identical per-prefix guarantees. If every prefix must pass the whole suite independently, batching cannot save those suite runs.
Numbers: a green pair saves one roughly 7.3-minute verification. A red batch followed by holder-only verification adds one such run. With independent per-leaf success p and equal suite costs, expected landed leaves per check is `[p + (n−1)p^n]/[2−p^n]`, versus p for solo. At p=.80, n=2 gives about 1.32× solo throughput and n=3 about 1.53×. Neither “1.3×” nor “2.8×” follows from p alone. Large all-waiting batches can approach half solo throughput in this simplified model. Correlated flakes and unequal runtimes further limit the estimate. Thus implement the requested cap-free policy, but do not also promise CPU never exceeds today's or infer waiting savings from mixed attempt/visit denominators.
Research: Julian Maurin, Mergify, [Dynamic merge queue batch size](https://mergify.com/blog/dynamic-merge-queue-batch-size), 2026-07-06, reports nearly doubling PRs per CI cycle during paired batches, but no increase in daily merges when demand was lower. It also discusses lost intermediate-commit guarantees. Their capped design is evidence of the tradeoff, not a recommendation to import their caps.
