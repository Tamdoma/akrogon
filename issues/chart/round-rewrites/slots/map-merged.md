# Merged map: round-rewrites

Merged by A on 2026-11-11 from slots/map-A.md and slots/map-B.md. Tags name the slot that wrote the line. A's later additions are marked "A, after B".

## Measurement

- (A) Wide reader, 28 claude door transcripts since 2026-10-17, rounds found by label: 82 answered rounds, 18 answered as first written (22%), 64 after one or more clarify messages. All 85 alias messages are `elid`.
- (B) Hand-checked recent cohort, 6 manual interviews since 2026-11-04, 36 requests: 33 of 35 completed requests needed clarification, 2 answered at once, 1 superseded. 40 clarify responses, 20,042 words, 45,168 output tokens. B did not reproduce the survey's 77 of 102 and 123,472 tokens.
- (A,B) Most rounds are not answered as first written. The two counts use different populations and are not added.
- (A) Size: first drafts answered directly have a median of 459 words, rewritten first drafts 671.5, the answered rewrites 458.5. Direct answers: 11 of 25 under 500 words, 6 of 39 from 500 to 800, 1 of 18 over 800. A first draft with Research, Pitfalls and Challenge check averages 806 words, 278 of them in those three parts.
- (B) Size is not the necessary change: 5 of 33 answered rewrites are longer, 14 rewritten first drafts carry none of the four labelled parts, and four answered rewrites keep all four. A 260-word draft was rewritten too. (A) agrees from its own data: 14 of 25 drafts under 500 words were rewritten.
- (B) What the rewrite supplies is decision context. M33: after 1252 → 497 words the operator still asked "where are they copied exactly, and what will 1b do?" and answered after a location table and a list of edits. M15: "why does it need to know the id?". M22: the two options became "every time, before each change goes live" against "only when you run them by hand".
- (A, after B) Haiku read of 21 rewritten pairs, 11 read in full: options and recommendations stay in all 21; what goes is file paths, numbers, research and pitfall lines and consultant tags; all 11 full reads show 1 to 3 process words replaced or explained, and 10 of 11 add a sentence of background. This supports B's reading.
- (A,B) Some rewrites change content, not only wording. (B) M15, M28, M32. (A, after B) pair 096 turns "share of time spent" into "time saved", pair 074 drops the recommended tags, pair 023 drops a fallback from an option. A rewrite is a risk as well as a cost.
- (A,B) Where the reply key sits does not separate answered from rewritten rounds.
- (A) Timing: 16 of 62 first aliases came within 30 s of the round and 24 within 60 s; the answer follows the rewrite in a median 75 s. Limit: reading during streaming is not counted.
- (B) Analogies fail: "box", then "fence", then "what does this fence even mean?" (M17).

## Causes

- K1 (B; A agrees after the pair read) The first draft is written from the door's side: process, mechanism, who found what. The rewrite is written from the operator's side: what changes for you, where, and what each option does. The existing register rule covers words, and doors apply it as word choice.
- K2 (A) Volume. The required parts push a full round to about 800 words, and 1 of 18 rounds over 800 words was answered directly. (B) Not sufficient alone: counterexamples above. (A) concedes it is a contributor, not the cause.
- K3 (A,B) A fixed second step, unproved. For: fast aliases, one session with 18 of 19 rounds rewritten, `elid` asks for "even more". Against: direct answers exist, and the operator's free-text requests name real gaps. Only the operator can say.
- K4 (A,B) Order and fork content: weak.
- (A,B) The records do not separate K1, K2 and K3. The operator's own account is the missing observation.

## Outside sources (read 2026-11-11)

- (A,B) Jakob Nielsen, NN/g, "Progressive Disclosure", 2006: show the few most important options first, get the split right, no more than two levels.
- (A) Jakob Nielsen, NN/g, "How Little Do Users Read?", 2008: at most 28% of the words read on an average visit. (B) Nielsen, "How Users Read on the Web", 1997: concise and scannable variants measured better.
- (B) Anna Kaley, NN/g, "Match Between the System and the Real World", 2018: use the user's concepts, not system terms. (B) Kate Moran, NN/g, "Usability Testing 101": observe the real participant on a real task.
- (A,B) Limits: web and interface studies, not a terminal interview; none sets a word limit; detail goes first when the reader must check evidence before acting.

- (B) A rewrite that changes an option is reported as a reshaped proposal, not as style.
- (A,B) A skill change reaches doors only at the next release link.

## After B's rebuttal (slots/map-rebuttal-B.md), accepted by A

- K1 corrected: the existing rule already asks for decision relevance ("each part saying only what the decision needs", and what changes depending on the answer). Open: whether doors apply that rule poorly, or a required part prevents applying it. Fork 1 leaves both possible.
- Order and fork content rank below K1 and are not dismissed. A final-position reply key is insufficient, which is not the same as placement having no effect. Fork 1's free text names "the choice itself needed changing" and "I could not find the important part".
- The operator's account is needed input, not proof: a rewrite also benefits from the first reading and sometimes changes options. "Not sure" and a combined answer are valid. The observation on a new substantive round stays before any rule edit, and one answered round is a bounded result.
