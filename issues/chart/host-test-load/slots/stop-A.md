# Notes A: stop (before reading B/C)
Pick: pause also stops. `akrogon pause` keeps its meaning (per repo, automatic dispatch off, src/pause.ts:49-58, gates at src/next.ts:715,827,930...) and additionally stops every leaf slice of that repo. Since the seat-scope boxes sit under one slice per leaf, that is one systemctl stop per running leaf slice of the repo. The existing pause is the redispatch fence: while paused, hooks, startup --resume and merge wake do nothing (docs/guide/next.md:106-117), so allocate never recreates the closed panes. `akrogon unpause` dispatches again, allocate recreates panes boxed, and seats restart from leaf artifacts. No new verb, no new leaf state.
Cost: pause becomes destructive. It kills seats mid-turn (worktrees and saved sessions stay). A per-leaf stop does not exist. A whole-host stop is `akrogon pause` per repo.
Rejected: a new stop verb plus a leaf-level stopped flag (new state field, new gate in every dispatch path, one more verb). Rejected: pause dispatch-only plus raw `systemctl --user stop`, since the next hook or startup pass recreates panes and redispatches at once.
Pi Esc: no change. Workers left by wait-abort stay in the pane box (manager.test.ts:1652 behavior is intentional) and die with the leaf stop.
Pitfalls: manual `akrogon next <target>` ignores pause by design (docs/guide/next.md:109), so the operator can restart a stopped leaf on purpose. Operator who wants a quiet pause without kills loses that, unless pause keeps a flag. Stop timing: systemd sends TERM then KILL after the scope's stop timeout, the run permit frees at holder death.
