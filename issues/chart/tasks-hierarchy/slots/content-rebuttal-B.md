# Slot B disagreements with content-merged

- **B / R1 / “Two seconds: what needs me and why (A,B,C)” overstates B's claim.** B recommends visible, evidence-backed attention reasons and explicitly says the contract cannot establish general human input/approval need. K1's “missing input” must mean the recorded missing artifact, not an unanswered human question. Likewise “prompt delivery error on an idle seat” should mean a recorded delivery error on a seat with no recorded busy timestamp. That does not establish the live agent's idle status. Evidence: `content-B.md` D3, F2, C1 and F9; `src/status.ts:undelivered`, `leafRed`; `model.d.ts:LeafJson`. Keep “Needs attention” distinct from “Needs me”.

- **B / R2 / K7 misattributes quiet treatment of the run warning to B.** B puts a waiting run among the board/system problems visible in two seconds, with raw table/run IDs on demand. It does not equate that run with a historical slow merge or a broken machine. `env.run` exists specifically when at least one run record is waiting, whereas `repo.slow` can be a historical verification record. The board must retain a visible waiting-run indication if the raw table leaves the routine scan. Do not silently demote both under a blanket quiet diagnostic category. Evidence: `content-B.md` C4, I5, I10 and Q6; `src/status.ts` envelope construction (`runs.some(record.state === 'waiting')`); `model.js:alerts`.

- **B / R3 / “A seat busy 3 hours is the stuck-agent signal” is too strong.** A busy duration can justify inspection, but the timestamp alone does not establish a stall or human intervention need. The envelope has a recorded start, without a progress/heartbeat field or an agreed duration threshold. Showing that duration is a valid option. Automatically assigning K1 or labeling it stuck requires a separate policy/evidence decision. N6 should retain this uncertainty, and N3's dispatch-age proposal should not be presented as proof of a stalled agent either. Evidence: `model.d.ts:LeafJson`; `src/status.ts:leafJson` copies state busy timestamps; `content-B.md` Q6 and D3.

- **B / R4 / Header count versus red dot needs a scope decision.** The proposed “count of rows needing the operator, equal to the panel-bar red dot” combines task and system scopes. `env.red` currently sums red leaves, unreadable entries and deploy failure. `/home/ivan/Work/infra/herdr-setup/mods/panel-bar/routine.js:redDot` is `envelope(result).red > 0`. A deploy failure can therefore light the dot with zero attention task rows. Keep that failure visible without inventing a task or calling the task count inconsistent. Decide whether the count is task-only or all problems, and keep the dot's meaning explicit. Evidence: `src/status.ts` envelope `red` expression; panel-bar `redDot`; `content-B.md` C1/C4 and I1.

- **B / R5 / N2's alternatives are not mutually exclusive.** Evidence-backed attention reasons and one producer-owned predicate can coexist. The actual choices are (1) which facts justify which category and (2) whether the current contract supports them or needs a producer change. Widening `leafRed` solely to make all indicators match does not answer the first question. Do not make intentional stop/pause, normal dependency waiting, or no recorded activity into failures just to obtain a common count. Preserve overlap between real problems and live activity. Evidence: `content-B.md` D2–D3, C1–C4 and Q3; `src/status.ts:leafRed` currently distinguishes failure, missing artifacts and undelivered prompts; `content-merged.md` K2 already retains overlapping attention/activity.
