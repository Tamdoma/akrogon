## R1 Binding constraint now

F1. Active time per leaf after 10-14 12:00 (V1 means, one visit each): implement 0.92 + check.review 0.07 + check.repair 0.08 + check.fix 0.50 x (14/20 visits) + merge 0.25 = about 1.7 h. A chain of depth 3 (M4) is then about 5 h of unavoidable serial time. Nothing inside that 1.7 h is a wait a program could remove at scale any more: the cheap check is 0.4 min, the only long runs are merge verifies and reproducing reruns (V3).

F2. What is left is time when no seat is working on the leaf at all: (i) `failed` dwell, 0.07-0.65 h only while a human was awake (V5), unbounded overnight; (ii) prerequisite wait, which is now the prerequisite's whole 1.7 h plus its own wait; (iii) empty intake, 4 open leaves of which 2 active (V7). With 2 active leaves at 1.7 h each the machine completes at most about 1.2 leaves per hour, so an 8 h unattended night can absorb roughly 10 ready leaves. Four are open. The constraint has moved from inside the leaf to the operator's presence: every event that needs a person (handoff review, `failed` recovery at phase.ts:241 "Operator recovery omits --slot") is synchronous with the operator being awake, while the machine runs 24 h.

F3. The single systemic flaw: there is no buffer of ready work in front of the unattended machine, and the one state that stops a leaf (`failed`) can only be released by a person. In Goldratt's terms the door is the drum and the leaves need a buffer sized to the night (drum-buffer-rope, S1); in Reinertsen's terms the attended handoff review is a fixed transaction cost, so its batch should grow until the buffer covers unattended capacity (E6 U-curve, S2). Arithmetic: a door pass that hands off 10 ready leaves before the operator leaves keeps throughput at about 1.2 leaves per hour overnight; today's 4 open leaves starve it after about 3 h. Number that would change this: if the door already hands off more than 10 ready leaves per evening and they still sit, the constraint is `failed` dwell alone.
