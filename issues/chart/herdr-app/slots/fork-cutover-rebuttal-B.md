# B: cutover rebuttal

- F1 “Zero open leaves … (all)” overstates my pick. I require zero old-runtime owners, including outstanding land/verify/broadcast work, and explicitly allow untouched pending contracts without legacy allocation. A door-built engine can avoid the bootstrap-seat problem if no lifecycle leaf is emitted for it. That is a specific execution choice, not evidence that every open folder is unsafe or that I endorsed that rule.
- F2 Q3 step 1 deletes the rollback guard before step 5 prunes pre-hc releases. I did not endorse that ordering. Keep refusal until legacy targets are actually removed and deployment/resume cannot restore a legacy previous pointer. A second hc release only changes the immediate previous target, not the permanent no-cross-cutover policy or the inventory of retained releases. Any guard removal needs that proof first.
- F3 “No herdr unit is listed” is too broad as a cleanup criterion. New herdr-app scopes or other desired hc/app units can contain that name. Identify obsolete units and running old-server executables specifically, remove those, and preserve the new runtime. An empty `command -v herdr` alone also does not prove old processes and reinstallation hooks are gone.
