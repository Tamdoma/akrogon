# Live change grant: independent round B

This round settles whether a recorded operator approval carries through a leaf's live work, repairs and verification. The intake reports about eight hours lost waiting for live-run authorization. The taken readiness contract already includes granted live changes, so the remaining choice is how precisely to define that grant and when it stops applying.

### 1 · Should seats reuse a scoped approval recorded by the door, require approval for every run, or treat handoff as broad permission?

A grant answers whether the operator permits a change. Credentials answer whether the provider permits it, and tool settings answer whether the seat can execute it. These are separate requirements. For example, approval to create and delete a test repository in a named organization does not authorize deleting another repository or using a personal account when the organization token fails.

Research: operator · [INTAKE.md](../INTAKE.md), I6, and taken [readiness-contract](../forks/readiness-contract.md), [key-creation](../forks/key-creation.md), [key-sheet](../forks/key-sheet.md), [env-source](../forks/env-source.md), read 2026-10-02 · Approval is an up-front input, grants belong in the contract, probes precede handoff, and producer writes have an explicit named destination. This supports reusable scoped authorization, not an unbounded handoff grant.

- **1a (recommended): one binding, scoped operator grant.** The door records explicit approval of named identities, accounts, targets, operations and limits in the authoritative readiness contract. Eligible seats reuse it for covered probes, implementation, repairs, reruns, merge checks and cleanup. A new approval is needed only when execution leaves those bounds or the grant expires or is revoked. This removes repeated approval of unchanged work without approving unrelated mutations.
- **1b: approval for each live execution.** Keep the contract's operation inventory, but attach authorization to an individual execution and require fresh approval for subsequent runs. This gives the operator a final decision on each execution and suits a deliberately imposed per-run approval requirement. It preserves the interruption and waiting cost seen in I6, including repair and cleanup runs, so it is not the default recommendation.
- **1c: broad handoff permission.** Treat approval to hand off the leaf or epic as permission for any live mutation the seat considers necessary. This is easiest to run but leaves accounts, targets and cleanup reach undefined. It conflicts with the taken contract's named grants and key-creation restrictions. A generic “implement this” cannot become authority to mutate arbitrary accounts.

Pitfalls: recorded authorization is not technical enforcement or proof of sufficient scopes. Conversely, an already recorded grant must not be relabeled as missing merely because a new seat, phase or repair pass begins.

Reply `1a`, `1b`, `1c`, or give a different rule. This round records no operator approval and performs no live operation.

## What the grant must name under 1a

1. **Approval provenance and scope:** a stable grant reference and revision, the operator who approved it, date, and the approval message or decision reference. Identify the registered repo and covered leaves. A single operator answer may approve the complete batch, but each leaf retains its precise scope. A recommendation or an agent-written approval flag is not approval.
2. **Executor and provider identity:** covered door/seat roles, provider and account/tenant/organization IDs, and the actual executing principal reference established by the proof. Reference the credential by its declared name and holding repo, never by value. A credential's name alone does not identify its principal. Grant reuse does not change skill ownership of work.
3. **Targets:** environment, resource IDs or names, hostnames, repository owner/name and relevant paths. For not-yet-created fixtures, specify an explicit target class: account, purpose, ownership marker, naming rule and allowed quantity. Record the resulting IDs before later changes or deletion. A matching prefix alone is not proof that an existing resource belongs to this leaf. A target outside this approved class requires approval.
4. **Operations and effects:** create, update, deploy, upload, migrate, delete, revoke or other actual mutation, with important parameter bounds and intended outcome. Include transitive helper calls, configured checks and test setup. Destructive, public-access, billing, DNS and retention effects must be explicit where they occur. Approval of a CLI name is insufficient when its arguments or called helpers select different operations.
5. **Repetition and recovery:** whether repair/re-proof runs, retries, resource recreation and rollback are covered, with the applicable resource, cost, data-loss or run limits. List cleanup operations and the owned resources they may affect. Approving creation must not silently imply deletion of pre-existing resources, and approving deletion must not imply permission to change a retention lock.
6. **Lifetime and conditions:** valid for the covered leaf lifecycle until completion or revocation, unless the operator names a shorter window or one-shot operation. Name any required prerequisite, approved rollout boundary or operator-imposed stop condition. A failed phase alone does not erase approval for an otherwise covered repair. Completion does not authorize an unrelated future leaf.
7. **Produced values:** carry key-creation 2a's exact authorized value name, purpose and canonical holding-repo destination, with the required revoke-on-save-failure action. The grant must not authorize an outside-account key-creating credential, which the taken key-creation 1a excludes. Env-source's canonical write rule remains binding.

Store this once in the structured readiness contract at the registered checkout. Plans and reports reference the grant and its revision rather than becoming independent approval sources. The door owns approval recording and amendment. A seat may propose a change, but cannot enlarge its own grant. This is workflow authority, not a claim that current filesystem permissions prevent edits.

`src/state.ts:35-58` currently has a strict schema with no grant or readiness field. `hand_built` at line 44, `debate`, reviewer verdicts and `failure` are not authorization. `readState` validates against that schema at lines 71-79. Implementing the taken contract therefore needs an explicit schema-supported location. Do not smuggle a grant into a failure reason or add arbitrary YAML that the current parser rejects. No extra grant command or separate approval database is needed for this recommendation.

## How a seat establishes that a run is inside the grant

Before its first covered mutation, and after a material change to the runner, inputs or grant, the seat reads the current authoritative contract and does the following:

1. Identify the proposed command or API operation, effective target configuration, executing identity and transitive mutations, including teardown. Compare their actual meaning against the grant. Use source inspection and non-secret target/identity results, not a command-text substring or a variable-name match.
2. Link that comparison to the existing real-identity proof for the same operation and target context. The door's probe record supplies identity, target, version, date, observed result and cleanup limits (`skills/chart-issues/SKILL.md:53`). Where identity or configuration is not established, establish it through the permitted identity/read checks before mutation. No grant allows pretending a presence check proves scopes.
3. Confirm role ownership and valid prerequisites. `check-issue` B still hands required live runs to A during repair (`skills/check-issue/SKILL.md:69`). A valid grant removes the need for new operator authorization, not this routing rule. Approved live work still follows cheap-proof-first and exit-status rules (`skills/implement-issue/SKILL.md:40-43`).
4. Record the grant reference/revision, source commit, operation, non-secret identity and target, relevant limits and why the run fits in the existing pass artifact. After execution, record result, created resource IDs and cleanup result. A rerun can reference unchanged scope evidence, but must account for newly created resources and remaining limits.
5. If the match cannot be established, do not perform the disputed mutation. Distinguish missing evidence or a repairable configuration defect from a genuine need for broader operator authority. Follow the phase's existing blocker route when only the operator can resolve it.

This is a thinking seat's semantic judgment backed by evidence. Akrogon can validate that a grant is present, valid and structurally complete. It cannot prove the effects of arbitrary subprocess code merely by checking a schema. No policy engine, command-spelling whitelist or new live API calls from akrogon are proposed.

## When approval is obtained or renewed

Obtain the initial grant during charting, before the first mutating proof. Key-sheet already requires approvals before affected proofs, so collecting it only at final handoff would be too late. Name both throwaway proof targets and intended execution targets where they differ. Final handoff confirms the recorded scope. Existing explicit operator authorization is carried forward, not asked for again.

New approval is required for a different executing principal or account, a target outside the approved selector, an added or different mutation, materially expanded side effects, a higher limit, an expired/revoked grant, or a changed condition that the existing grant does not cover. A credential replacement that demonstrably preserves the same principal and approved authority needs renewed identity/scope evidence, not automatically new operator consent. Routine code changes, a new seat, phase transition, ordinary covered retry or repair do not require approval if their effects remain within the recorded bounds.

A provider 403 is not permission to switch identities. A denied tool call is not permission to use a different route around the deny. Cleanup that requires an unapproved deletion, retention change or scope expansion is a blocker, not implied recovery authority.

## Pitfalls and related forks

- **Permission layers:** a grant supplies consent. It supplies neither credential scopes nor effective tool access. A passing proof supplies evidence, not authorization for a new mutation.
- **Transitive effects and cleanup:** deploy/test commands can create extra resources. “Run tests” is not enough scope. Cleanup inventories must include deletion and revocation without silently permitting new retention or security-policy changes.
- **Unknown outcome and retries:** a failed reply can hide a successful creation. Discover owned resources before retrying, and keep aggregate effects inside the grant's limits. A repeated command can have a different effect.
- **Mutable records:** branch prose, report claims and a seat-written boolean cannot enlarge an approved grant. Use the authoritative contract and approval provenance. This workflow remains dependent on seat judgment and current credential/tool boundaries.
- **Fixture choice:** proof-fixtures remains open. This round defines the information a fixture grant requires, but does not choose shared versus disposable fixtures or authorize specific targets. Required mutating proofs wait until their target and cleanup scope are known and approved.
- **Blocker mechanics:** blocker-record remains open. This round does not invent new failed-state syntax or permission-rule edits. Until that fork settles, preserve the existing phase-specific operator-action handling. Env producer-write permissions also remain there, as env-source records.

## Challenge check

The strongest objection is that a recorded grant does not technically stop a mistaken or compromised seat from exceeding it when the same credential has broader reach. This proposal addresses repeated consent and accountability, not credential isolation. Enforceable least privilege remains at the provider/tool boundary, and material uncertainty about identity or effects blocks the disputed operation.

A grant also cannot approve every future test fixture by an unconstrained prefix. It can authorize a bounded, owned fixture class or named targets, with evidence linking actual IDs to that class. If those bounds cannot be stated before the required proof, hold that proof and resolve proof-fixtures rather than broadening this grant by inference.
