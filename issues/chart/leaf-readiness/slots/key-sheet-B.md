# Key sheet: independent round B

Research date: 2026-10-02. This round uses the named intake, taken locks, key-sheet Question and Carries, chart Take/Handoff rules, status and dispatch code, and the outside sources below. No other slot files or env files were read. No provider calls or lifecycle commands were run.

## Question

Q1. When and where does the operator get exact step-by-step instructions for creating each missing key and other operator-supplied input, and at what point in the flow are they done?

The taken decisions constrain the answer: every need is visible before handoff; the operator creates missing outside-account keys; agents do not hold key-creating credentials; declared leaf-produced keys are stored by their producer under the agreed write authorization. The readiness contract covers keys, scopes, files, hostnames, approvals and producer outputs. Akrogon does not make live provider calls.

## Options

1. **O1. Show the complete sheet at the door only.** The door researches and presents the batch before probes, the operator completes it, and final handoff reviews the results. Status lists gaps and refers the operator back to the door for instructions. This has the least CLI presentation work, but instructions disappear into the conversation unless retained. It also makes later repair depend on finding or repeating that conversation. Showing the sheet only at the final handoff review is too late for required probes.

2. **O2. Show the sheet at the door before probes, retain the instructions in the readiness contract, and print unresolved instructions through status. Recommended.** The door owns research and the initial attended batch. The same contract carries the instructions into the leaf. Default status lists gaps across open leaves and shows the corresponding creation or repair steps below the table. Leaf-specific status shows that leaf’s full input instructions and proof references. This gives the operator both timely help and a durable way to retrieve it, using the status surface already selected in the readiness lock. It needs no separate instruction command or provider adapter.

3. **O3. Keep the contract inventory and use a separate instruction command.** A command could print the same stored recipes on demand, or ask an agent to research them then. Printing stored content adds another way to discover information already needed in status. Researching only on demand discovers setup work too late and cannot reliably finish the pre-handoff probes. A separate command has no demonstrated benefit here over existing default and leaf-specific status.

## Recommendation and exact timing

Choose O2. The operator does the missing-input batch during charting, after the door has resolved enough design choices to identify the complete operation and input inventory, and before the door runs the required real-identity probes. Final handoff review confirms completion and evidence. It is not the first occasion on which the operator sees setup instructions.

1. **A1. Discover and declare.** The door derives requirements from the selected design, inspected consumer code and tools, and every direct, transitive, fixture and cleanup operation. It identifies existing reusable inputs, missing inputs and declared producer outputs. A genuinely human-only prerequisite is named with its owner as soon as discovered, as Take already requires. Conflicting requirements or unknown scopes remain chart questions rather than placeholders for the implementer.
2. **A2. Present one actionable batch.** Before asking the operator to create anything, the door shows all operator actions across the proposed leaves, including steps, destinations, required authority and proof expectations. Group compatible actions once, with their consuming leaves listed. Do not combine inputs merely because they have the same variable name. The operator creates or repairs outside-account inputs and completes approvals using the supplied steps. Secrets go directly into the declared holding repo’s gitignored env, not into chat. Approvals needed for probes must be completed before those probes.
3. **A3. Prove the supplied inputs.** The door uses the identity the leaf will use, not the operator’s broader dashboard identity, for the operation proofs. Record identity and target references, operation, inputs without secret values, date/version, result, cleanup and limits. A failed scope or target proof produces a specific repair action in the same sheet. The door researches the correction and the operator supplies it before handoff. “One batch” means one complete upfront inventory, not a claim that failed probes cannot require correction.
4. **A4. Review and hand off.** The attended final review presents the contracts, completed operator actions, granted live changes, proof evidence and declared producer relationships. Missing operator prerequisites, required approvals or insufficient proofs hold the affected handoff. Peer contract review and final writes follow the existing Handoff rule. The door finishes without running next.
5. **A5. Dispatch and later repair.** After handoff, status prints gaps and their stored instructions. Next checks the declared readiness before allocating a worktree or starting a seat. A later removal, expiry or changed grant sends the operator back to the specific repair steps and the door for any required renewed proof. Status neither creates inputs nor declares a live permission valid from a presence check.

The initial batch cannot depend on status finding open leaves: today its leaf scanner reads issues/open, while contracts are still drafts before handoff. The door must therefore render the draft contract’s instructions itself. Saving the final contract makes later status retrieval possible. This recommendation does not require teaching status to discover chart drafts or creating unfinished open leaves just to print a setup list.

## Where exact instructions come from

The door writes them. Provider-specific requirements come from current primary documentation for the actual selected credential type and operation, supplemented by inspected installed tooling and consumer code. Operator-supplied account, domain and organizational facts resolve the target. Named practitioner advice informs the procedure and pitfalls, but cannot establish an account’s actual grants. Provider names are content in the contract, not hard-coded akrogon branches.

For each operator action, the contract retains the input’s purpose, consuming leaves, owner, identity/target, required permissions or acceptance condition, exact holding repo and destination, numbered steps, source link or local source location, checked date and relevant tool version, and expected nonsecret completion evidence. Steps may reference an existing authoritative procedure instead of copying it, but must give the exact local choices and values needed to apply it. A documentation homepage or “create a token with the right scopes” is not an actionable procedure. Free prose is suitable for steps. Mechanical checks should validate required content and valid states, not demand particular wording.

| Input | What the steps must settle |
|---|---|
| Key or login | Account and credential type, authority required to create it, exact permissions and resource selection, appropriate lifetime, destination variable and holding repo, one-time secret handling, any organization approval, and the operation proof that follows. No instruction to give agents a creating credential. |
| Scope or account access | Who can grant it, which existing identity is being changed, exact target and required permission, why it is needed, how completion is evidenced, and which proof becomes invalid after the change. |
| File or registry | Inspected source template or creation procedure, exact destination, required consumer fields and target selections, expected validation, and whether it is an operator input or a leaf output. An example file is not proof that a real account registry exists. |
| Hostname or other value | Who chooses or controls it, required properties, exact configuration destination, and any ownership, DNS or reachability evidence required by the operations. Choosing a string is distinct from authorizing or configuring the domain. |
| Approval | Named approver, exact operations and targets including cleanup, where the authorization is recorded, and how the door knows it is complete. A token’s permissions do not authorize a live change. |
| Produced input | Named producing leaf, output destination and accepted completion evidence. Its instructions identify the producer rather than telling the operator to fabricate its output. Secret writes retain the taken authorization and failed-save revocation rule. |

No new credential, secret-bearing file, command output or secret URL is embedded in a contract or status output. Instructions show names and destinations, not values. The CLI prints the stored procedure and local gaps. The agent refreshes the procedure when needed. The CLI does not browse dashboards, research scopes or execute the procedure.

## Pitfalls and limits

1. **P1. Instructions arrive after the point at which they are needed.** Final handoff review alone, or status after writing open leaves, is too late for the mandatory proofs. Present the complete draft sheet before probes and reserve final review for completion and evidence.
2. **P2. Exact dashboard steps become stale.** Record the official source and checked date. Distinguish document-checked instructions from a dashboard sequence actually observed. On resumed work or a reported mismatch, the door rechecks the relevant source or permitted live surface before telling the operator to proceed. Do not guess new button names, broaden grants or silently substitute another credential type. A recent document date is not proof of an authenticated UI walkthrough. No dashboard was inspected in this round.
3. **P3. A completed setup checklist is mistaken for an operation proof.** Token activity, file existence and an approval request do not prove target access, usable configuration or completed authorization. Changed identity, target or operation invalidates the corresponding evidence. Presence checks remain local and akrogon makes no live calls. Known expired or revoked credentials require repair even when nonempty.
4. **P4. Producer outputs conflict with pre-handoff proof timing.** The taken lock allows declared outputs to wait on their producer. That does not waive Take’s real-identity, target-bound pre-handoff probe rule. If a dependent operation requires a not-yet-created identity or target and no sufficient permitted probe exists, hold that dependent’s handoff until the producer delivers it, then prove it. Do not label a different identity’s or target’s test as sufficient, or silently defer required proof to the dependent seat. The overall inventory can still be shown up front. If simultaneous dependent handoff is required, this is a material rule conflict to settle explicitly before handoff.
5. **P5. Shared names conceal different requirements.** Deduplicate an operator action only when identity, target, holding repo, permissions and destination match. Include fixture and cleanup needs before asking for grants. A single broad key is not the automatic solution to divergent requirements.
6. **P6. Persisting a recipe is confused with persisting a secret.** Contracts and status may expose account identifiers and operational plans, but must not include secret values, copied dashboard responses or secret-bearing command arguments. The operator supplies outside-account secrets directly to the declared destination. Leaf-produced secrets use the already-taken authorized write behavior.

This round defines the proposed flow and instruction surface. It does not certify any consumer inputs, scopes, approvals or operation proofs. Those must be established during the attended setup and probe sequence before the affected handoff.
