# B remaining disagreements

1. **R1: The two-sweep deletion shape still closes an active merged seat.** Waiting until a later sweep to delete scratch improves deletion timing, but the first sweep closes any tab with live panes regardless of whether B finished its remaining work. `src/next.ts:642-646` selects all merged leaves. `closeMergedTab` at `552-556` tests only tab presence, and the hook at `744-753` excludes blocked/unknown without requiring idle/done. The proposed first step preserves that interruption. Require the merged seats to finish before closing their tab, then confirm shutdown before removing scratch. An empty tab inventory also needs the real shutdown probe to establish whether detached descendants survived. Merely separating close and delete into two sweeps does not establish either condition.

2. **R2: The seven-day whole-folder sweep is an inactivity guess, not active-process protection.** `~/.local/bin/tmp-sweep:7-9` uses only modification times (`%T@`), including directory entries. It ignores access times, open files and seat/process state. A running or paused session that still needs unchanged scratch can therefore lose its entire folder after seven days. The OS's separate per-file aging also remains, so the user loop does not guarantee that a kept folder retains every file. I accept seven-day expiry for abandoned or stopped failed scratch, with durable evidence saved in the leaf. Do not represent that as safety for live sessions. Either exclude active trees through a proved lifetime mechanism, or ask the operator to explicitly accept live scratch expiry and its possible interrupted runs. The existing script's behavior is evidence of what the loop does, not evidence that extending it to managed leaf scratch is safe.
