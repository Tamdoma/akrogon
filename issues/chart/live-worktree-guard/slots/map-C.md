## 3. Material forks

### Q1. How should cleanup know a store worktree holds live work?

- O1. Read the chart (direct attempts only): reconcile scans `issues/chart/*/CHART.md` in the repo it is sweeping, parses each `Direct attempt` section's `worktree:` and `branch:` lines (shapes.md:41-53), and keeps that path while the chart's last end marker is not `Closed`/`Held` (last marker authoritative, shapes.md:55) and no landed SHA is recorded, always under the process veto of O4. Cost: reconcile learns one markdown section format, parsed every pass; the door updates the section "through landing" in prose, so a lagging or malformed section silently loses protection; the step-1 race remains (door creates the worktree and writes the section in the same step, order not fixed, chart-issues SKILL.md Direct route step 1). Invites: parser drift as shapes evolve; a second reader of chart prose beside the status chart rows (src/status.ts chartRow already regex-parses CHART.md markers, so precedent exists). Breaks later if a chart can live in a different repo than its direct worktree's destination (fog G1): that repo's reconcile sees no chart.
- O2. Creator-written claim record: whoever creates a store worktree writes `<store>/<name>.owner` (kind, chart path or owner identity, branch, created) *before* `git worktree add`, mirroring the batch owner record fixed at `<store>/batch-<id>.owner` written before the copy (scratch-copy-removal design.md:70) and the run-dir `.owner` precedent. Reconcile keeps a claimed worktree, deletes a claim whose worktree is gone, and expires a direct claim only against its chart's end state. Cost: the two prose creators (door, seat A for workers) must write the record by skill text alone, or a small akrogon helper must do it; pid-style liveness is wrong authority here because a direct attempt is designed to survive session replacement (chart-issues SKILL.md repair bound keeps the count in CHART.md for that reason), so the record is a claim to cross-check, not a death test. Invites: stale claims after a crashed door keep worktrees forever until the chart end is checked; every future creator must know to claim, which is exactly the knowledge the last two incidents show arrives after the first deletion.
- O3. Git-native lock: the door runs `git worktree lock --reason "direct attempt <chart>"` immediately after creating the worktree and unlocks at the existing remove step (Direct route landing step 8); reconcile learns to read the `locked` line of `git worktree list --porcelain` (already fetched at src/reconcile.ts:165-172) and keep with git's reason instead of attempting removal each pass. Cost: protection still depends on one prose step, and a crashed door leaves a locked zombie only a human unlocks; but it works on today's origin/main code with zero reconcile change (F8), travels with the worktree across repos and sessions, and any operator or future tool can protect a manual worktree the same way today. Invites: a later leaf generalizing `--force` removal in the fallthrough branch would silently defeat it (a locked worktree needs `--force` twice, scratch-copy-removal P1 limits), so the lock's protection is only as durable as the rule never to force-remove unknown kinds.
- O4. Process evidence only: extend the fallthrough branch with the `/proc` cwd/open-fd veto #93 already took for its deletions (store-hygiene Taken Q2). Measured tonight this catches the running verify (F4) and any shell or editor sitting in the worktree, and herdr `pane process-info` cannot substitute (foreground job only, F4). Cost: a same-uid `/proc` scan per pass and self-clearing false keeps (an `rg` or file manager inside the path). Breaks nothing, but alone it does not fix the incident: between commands, while B reviews or the door thinks, no process is inside and removal proceeds. It is a necessary veto, not a liveness source.
- O5. Move direct worktrees out of the swept roots: direct attempts (and only they) get a new root the sweep does not enumerate. Cost: a second managed place to clean someday, `worktree_store` stops meaning "where worktrees live", every reader of the store (status kept lines, leftovers) misses direct attempts, and operator worktrees in the store stay exposed. It evades the sweep instead of fixing its knowledge, and store-sweep's host step would eventually claim the new root as junk.
- My pick: O1 as the authority for direct attempts, with O4 as the universal veto on any removal, and O3 adopted immediately as a door step on main to protect the attempt that is live tonight before any leaf merges. Reason: the chart already is the attempt's record of record, designed to survive exactly the session replacements that defeat pid and pane signals, it protects the in-flight script-lane-effort attempt with no door behavior change, and its end state is already defined (last marker, landed SHA). O2 becomes necessary only if G1 shows charts can live outside the destination repo; then the claim file is the pointer that carries the chart path across repos, and O1 alone is insufficient.

### Q2. What happens to a registered store worktree nobody can account for (an operator's manual one, a future tool's)?

- O1. Keep sweeping it as today, once clean, reachable, pane-free and (with Q1/O4) process-free. The store is akrogon's managed space (F9); known kinds protect themselves by chart, claim, lock or leaf state. Cost: the next new creator repeats this incident once before it learns to register, which is the exact pattern of a77c260 then #94. An operator who parks a manual worktree in the store between commands loses its ignored files and setup (commits and tracked changes are protected by `worktreeRemovable`, F2).
- O2. Invert the default for registered worktrees: removal needs affirmative finished proof (a known kind in a finished state); anything else is kept and shown with a reason naming how to make it removable or claim it. This kills the whole class for every future creator and matches store-hygiene's own taken principle for its items ("unprovable items stay, recorded with the reason", Q3). Cost: it reverses a pinned behavior (tests/reconcile.test.ts:493-504 sweeps a registered variant) and junk variants accumulate until a human removes them, against the operator's clean-folders direction in #93 Round 2 (`5` answer); `akrogon leftovers` becomes the standing list a human works through. - O3. Age or idle-time removal for unknowns (no process, no pane, untouched for N days). Foreclosed by lock: store-hygiene Taken Q3 says nothing is deleted for age, and a growth-stopped direct attempt is intentionally kept across days (chart-issues SKILL.md growth stop), so age cannot tell live from junk here.
- My pick: O2 for *registered* worktrees, O1's current treatment retained only for the named scratch kinds #93 already decided. Reason: a registered worktree is the one store entry that is definitionally somebody's checkout, its recreation cost measured tonight is a 2.6 GB reinstall plus a broken in-flight command, and the keep is visible and reversible by hand, while a wrong removal mid-run is neither. ### Q3. When does a direct attempt stop being live, so cleanup may take its worktree?

- O1. Never by reconcile: the door always removes it (landing step 8, abandon under growth stop), and reconcile only keeps and reports an ended attempt's leftover for a human. Safest, and matches the door-owns-cleanup shape of the protocol; cost is that a door crashing between push and step 8, or after `Closed` is written, leaves a full worktree until a human reads status.
- O2. Reconcile may remove it when the chart's last marker is `Closed` or `Held` and the usual guards pass (clean, reachable, no pane, no process). This is crash cleanup only, since the door removes before writing `Closed` (landing steps 8-9 order). `Handed off` must not qualify: after a growth-stop handoff the leaf with the same slug adopts the worktree (chart-issues SKILL.md growth stop), and in the gap before dispatch records `state.worktree` the candidate rules already treat a slug-matching worktree as that active leaf's (src/reconcile.ts:362-379).
- O3. Reconcile may remove it once a landed SHA is recorded in the section. Earlier than O2 by minutes, but the door still runs source closes, broadcast and record writes in that window (landing steps 6-9) and a pushed-but-unclosed attempt may still need the worktree for a second push attempt after a rejected first push (step 5 allows 2 attempts, each with fresh rebase and checks in the worktree). Landed SHA is written only after step 8 in the shape (shapes.md:53 "after landing"), so in practice O3 collapses into O2 or races the door.
- My pick: O2. Reason: the last marker is already the authoritative chart state (shapes.md:55), so reconcile needs no new end signal, and restricting reconcile to after-the-door cleanup keeps one remover per attempt in the normal path.

## 4. Practitioner questions and pitfalls over the work's lifetime

- P1. Check-then-remove race: any signal (chart read, pane list, /proc scan) is stale the moment it is taken; a command can start inside the worktree, or the door can create the worktree, between the check and `git worktree remove` (src/reconcile.ts:208-213). What removes it: the creator-side record written before the worktree exists (claim file Q1/O2, or the Direct attempt section written before `git worktree add` if step 1's order is fixed that way), plus a second process check immediately before removal; the git lock (Q1/O3) is the only check git itself enforces atomically at remove time.
- P2. Session replacement of the door mid-attempt. Panes, pids and process cwd all die with the replaced session while the attempt lives on by design (repair count kept in CHART.md so it survives replacement, chart-issues SKILL.md repair bound). What removes it: only signals stored outside the session qualify as liveness (chart section, claim file, git lock); pid-based records copied from the batch pattern would expire a live attempt whose door was merely replaced.
- P3. Stale protection after a crash. A door that dies after push but before removing the worktree, or abandons without writing `Held`, leaves a chart that still reads live and a worktree nobody will remove. What removes it: Q3/O2's end-marker rule plus the kept reason naming the chart in status, so the zombie is visible and attributed rather than anonymous junk; nothing may auto-expire it by age (store-hygiene lock).
- P4. Chart prose drift. The Direct attempt section is model-written markdown updated in place through landing; a renamed heading, a second attempt appended as a new section, or a worktree path edited after `git worktree move` silently unprotects or misprotects. What removes it: parse only the two lines shapes.md fixes (`branch:`, `worktree:` at :43-44) inside the last `Direct attempt` section (the same last-wins convention as end markers, shapes.md:55), treat any parse ambiguity as keep-and-report rather than remove, and keep the process veto as backstop.
- P5. Future creators will not know the rule. Workers (2026-10-14) and direct attempts (2026-10-15) each learned by deletion. What removes it: whatever Q1/Q2 decide must be stated where a creator looks, meaning the store's contract in `src/AREA.md` beside reconcile and the keep reason text itself naming how to claim a worktree (for example `unknown registered worktree; lock it or record it in a chart to keep it`), because status kept lines are the one surface every operator reads.
- P6. False keeps from the process veto. A stray `rg`, editor server, file manager or Chrome with an open file inside pins a dead worktree, and a same-uid `/proc` scan misses other users' processes entirely (store-hygiene scopes its scan to this uid). What removes it: the veto records the pid as the reason (the #93 taken shape), so the keep is self-explaining and self-clearing; other-user processes are out of akrogon's single-user host assumption today and should be named as a limit, not engineered around.
- P7. Herdr pane cwd lag and pane-list failure. Pane cwd is herdr's cached view; a pane that just `cd`'d into the worktree may still report the old cwd for a sample, and `reconcileNeedsPanes` returning false passes an empty list that judges as "no panes" rather than "unknown" (src/next.ts:1336 passing `[]`, src/reconcile.ts candidate branch treating only `live === null` as unavailable). What removes it: every new candidate kind must be counted by `reconcileNeedsPanes` (already a store-hygiene taken rule, Q3), and a registered-worktree candidate must count even when it is the only candidate.
- P8. Two doors, one store. Two charts in the same repo can run direct attempts at once (live tonight: framework store also held other leaf worktrees while script-lane-effort ran), and nothing stops two charts choosing the same slug except git's branch collision at `git worktree add`. What removes it: the keep lookup must be by worktree path and branch from all chart sections, not by chart, and a path claimed by two charts is keep-and-report, never a parse error that falls through to removal.
- P9. Testability of the dangerous path. The fake herdr holds one status per pane and no process tree (tests/fake-herdr.ts agent wait/list), and reconcile tests run real git worktrees in temp stores (tests/reconcile.test.ts dispatchFixture). What removes it: chart-reading and claim rules are pure filesystem inputs and testable in the existing fixture; the process veto needs the helper seam scratch-copy-removal already introduces (its design.md:68), and a test that plants a chart folder with a live and an ended Direct attempt in the fixture repo is the regression case for this incident.
- P10. Locked-worktree drift. If Q1/O3's git lock is adopted even as an interim, the door's own landing step 8 (`git worktree remove`) will fail until unlocked, and a future leaf that generalizes `--force` in `removeWorktree` silently defeats every lock. What removes it: the unlock belongs in the same protocol step that removes, and the no-force-on-unknown-kinds rule should be written where `removeWorktree` lives, as a comment the next editor meets.

## 6. Fog

- G1. Where a chart folder lives when the door's repo and the direct destination repo differ. Shapes records chart paths relative to the authoritative registered root from `akrogon config` (shapes.md:3) and the incident chart lives in the destination repo itself, but I did not establish whether a door opened in repo A can run a direct attempt into repo B while its chart stays in A. If it can, Q1/O1 alone cannot protect that worktree and a claim or lock must carry the chart pointer across repos. Confidence: low; needs one read of the door's destination handling or a door answer.
- G2. How long a direct worktree may legitimately sit with no pane, no process and no chart edit (operator away mid-attempt for days, growth stop awaiting a new fork). Every signal except the chart/claim/lock decays in that window, and I cannot yet state sharply what distinguishes it from an abandoned attempt the operator forgot, other than the chart not being ended.
- G3. Whether any other live creator already writes into the store without a record (older akrogon versions' worktrees on other hosts, operator scripts, other harnesses' own worktree features pointed at the store). The inventory in F7 covers this repo's code and skills only; the answer decides how much weight Q2/O2's accumulate-junk cost really carries.
- G4. What `git worktree remove` does to a process whose cwd is inside on this platform beyond deleting the directory (the seed reports directory loss mid-run; whether running processes then fail on next write, keep working on deleted inodes, or corrupt partial outputs likely differs per tool). It changes the urgency wording, not the fix shape.
