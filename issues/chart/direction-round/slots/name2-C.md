Read: issues/closed/charting-vocabulary/rename-vocabulary/brief.md (one land vocabulary: territory, map, fog, fork, question, round, chart, off route), README.md, docs/start/what-it-is.md (chart, leaf, seat, phase, worktree, landing; the door, the direct route). 1. **stakes**. Step: "drive the stakes"; record: "the stakes" (`forks/stakes.md`); a fork the operator keeps is "staked". A surveyor drives stakes into the ground before the map is drawn, and every later line is measured from them. The word also carries "what is at stake", which is the priorities half, and "to stake a claim", which is the votes half: a staked fork stays the operator's. Short, plain, one syllable like fork, fog, map.

2. **cairns**. Step: "raise the cairns"; record: "the cairns" (`forks/cairns.md`); a fork with a cairn is the operator's. On open land cairns stand at the forks so a walker in fog keeps the route; they are set once by whoever knows the ground and followed by everyone after. It fits fog and fork exactly. Cost: a less common word, and it leans toward the votes half more than the priorities half.

3. **landmarks**. Step: "fix the landmarks"; record: "the landmarks" (`forks/landmarks.md`). Landmarks are what a traveller steers by across the whole territory, set before the map is read; a priority is a landmark the run keeps in sight. Cost: it names what to steer by but not which forks stay the operator's, so the vote marking needs a second word.

Favorite: **stakes**. It is the only one that names both halves in one plain word, it fits the surveying side of chart-making, and "staked fork" reads at a glance in a round.
