### G2 · If two priorities conflict, how should A choose between them?

- a) **Recommended:** record their order and any explicit hard limit. For example, optimize impact first and prefer speed when outcomes are otherwise comparable. Do not invent a budget or turn a preference into a prohibition.
- b) Keep priorities unordered and let A judge each tradeoff separately.
- c) Ask the operator to resolve each new conflict in attended mode, and have A record a separate resolution under autopilot.

Reshapes: answer format, the questions asked about acceptable sacrifices, later recommendation reasons, and correction handling. A rank costs a little opening effort but reduces repeated ambiguity. Unordered values are easier to give but can support opposite picks. Evidence cannot decide which of the operator's competing values should win.

### G3 · How much should a direction vote settle?

- a) **Recommended:** settle intent, priorities and explicit limits. Use them to discover and order forks and recommend options. Preserve material technical choices for their normal rounds, with A answering those rounds under autopilot.
- b) Let direction also settle later choices when the implication is clear, recording why a separate choice is unnecessary.

Reshapes: the opening map, what counts as an open fork, how priorities enter later carries, and the evidence used to claim a fork settled. Option a keeps the votes influential while making the boundary visible. Option b shortens the process but risks treating “prefer simple” as an answer to an unasked technical tradeoff. Existing process describes fork answers; choosing how much new direction may replace them is a control preference, not a fact evidence can settle. Neither option broadens autopilot's approval authority.

### G4 · How should the process show what the votes changed?

- a) **Recommended:** give one short account after the answers of what the process will prioritize and what it will give up. Refer to the governing priority in later recommendations when it changes the choice.
- b) Keep the answers in the record and show their effect through the map and later choices without a separate explanation.
- c) Compare every material option against every priority.

Reshapes: the light round's closing response, map explanations, later recommendation prose and review criteria. Option a makes steering visible at a small reading cost. Option b is lighter but makes neglected priorities harder to spot. Option c adds recurring work and can invite invented scores. Evidence cannot settle how much visibility the operator wants in exchange for reading effort.
