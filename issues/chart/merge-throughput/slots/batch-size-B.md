# Batch size: blind notes, Slot B

## Direct answer to the operator

Two was a cautious starting size, not a proven best size. The elegant change is to let successful full batches increase the repo's limit slowly and failed batches decrease it quickly, while keeping the existing failure narrowing. Queue length supplies available work. It should not decide how much risk to combine in one run. A limit that only shrinks optimizes avoiding red batches until it eventually abandons batching altogether.

### What the pass-rate model actually says

For `n` total leaves, assuming independent equal pass probability `p`, all-or-nothing landing, and equal run cost, expected leaves landed in this one run are `n × p^n`. The continuous maximum is `-1 / ln(p)` for `0 < p < 1`. Check the neighboring integers, respect the available queue, and use at least one leaf. More directly, size `n+1` beats `n` exactly when `p > n/(n+1)`. If `p=1`, take all available work under this simplified model. If `p=0`, every size lands zero.

| Assumed p | Best total size | Expected leaves/run at that size | Expected leaves/run at size 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.62 | 2 | 0.769 | 0.769 |
| 0.86 | 7 | 2.435 | 1.479 |

These are arithmetic illustrations using the seed's rates, not measured Akrogon optima. Two wins over solo when `p > 0.5`, but three wins over two when `p > 2/3`. Shared base failures, interactions between leaves, unequal runtimes and failure-narrowing work invalidate the simple optimum. A green stack can even mask one member's defect. Thus neither a fixed 2 nor a fitted `p` proves the best actual throughput. The real target is landed leaves divided by total merge-attempt time, including failed and narrowing attempts, with the same final gate.

Measured check: framework `issues/log.jsonl` has 25 merged and 26 merge-to-fix exits on 2026-10-08, and 21 merged and 12 merge-to-fix exits on 2026-10-09. Those ratios are 49% and 64%, not the seed's 62% and 86% solo rates. This does not disprove the reported solo subset. Existing phase records do not label solo versus carried members, and green batches emit one completion per member (`src/phase.ts:474-477`), while splits emit no phase transition (`:788-799`). Do not use either aggregate as independent per-leaf p. The taken attempt records provide a better outcome/time denominator once implemented, not historical gate timing now.

### The loops and their wrong targets

- L1, reinforcing waste: longer queue → whole-queue first batch → higher chance of a red stack → extra serial runs and queue age → still longer queue. The closure is unlimited follower selection `src/next.ts:1017-1020`, with the per-holder reduction discarded on leaving merge `src/phase.ts:154`. This makes more accumulated work increase exposure rather than capacity.
- L2, reinforcing age: wait → conflicts → persistent solo exclusion → fewer leaves carried per run → longer wait. Closure: solo write `src/next.ts:1181`, exclusion `:1019`. The approved clean-run clearing rule addresses this. A conflict does not itself reset queue age.
- L3, balancing the wrong target: halving followers retains the holder and narrows until it passes or fails solo (`src/phase.ts:788-799`). It protects a finite route to resolving the holder, not the fastest landing of every good member. If the holder is defective, good followers are repeatedly tested with it. An excluded bad follower can poison a future batch. This is a limitation, not proof that holder-preserving split is nonterminating.
- L4, incomplete capacity recovery: approved start-2/halve/floor-1/no-growth moves total capacity to one after a red pair and never increases it. This rule is recorded in `forks/first-package.md`, Taken, not implemented in current code. It balances failure exposure with no counteracting success feedback. A growth rule supplies the missing recovery.
- L5, corrective re-entry versus queue age: bounce → repair → new merge stamp → additional waiting/drift. `src/phase.ts:152`; `src/routing.ts:34-35`. Returning repaired work behind waiting work protects others from one recurring defect, but does not minimize repaired-leaf lead time. Approved dependency priority changes the next selection, not the running holder. Do not reopen that policy as an incidental batch fix.

## Q1 five-part blind notes

1. **Pick, reason and cost:** reopen only the approved no-growth clause. One persisted repo window `W`, defined as TOTAL leaves, starts at 2. Select `min(W, eligible available leaves)` without waiting to fill. After a successfully landed full window, set `W := W+1`, once per successful attempt, not once per member. An underfilled success leaves W unchanged. After a non-base red attempt testing n leaves, set `W := max(1, floor(n/2))`; base-held outcomes leave W unchanged. Retain holder-preserving narrowing, but retry only the original failed batch's saved members, at most W total including holder. No new arrivals fill a diagnostic retry. At one total leaf, a red result takes the already-approved counted repair path. Reason: a small extension restores batching after failures, uses observed outcomes, and reuses the working stack/restore machinery. Cost: some deliberate growth probes fail, and bad-holder narrowing still costs serial runs. This is an adaptive heuristic, not a proven optimum. Growth on successful full singleton attempts permits recovery from W=1. Underfilled queue successes cannot inflate W while idle.

2. **Rejected options:** fixed 2 forever makes high-yield queues needlessly serial, though it is simpler if the operator chooses predictability over exploration. Halve-only permanently loses batching. Whole-queue sizing lets queue pressure amplify failures. A computed p optimum requires reliable standalone probability estimates and ignores correlation and narrowing costs. Full bisection introduces pending sub-batches, ownership changes, interaction-sensitive attribution and extra gates to prove both sides. Ejecting a “culprit” from a single red aggregate run invents attribution. A reproducible culprit can justify repair after proper evidence, but building general culprit discovery is a second outcome. Keep the existing split now, and use the approved attempt evidence to decide whether its measured waste justifies reopening it later.

3. **Evidence, tier, source and date:** operator tier, `forks/first-package.md`, `red-main-hold.md`, `bounce-counting.md`, `queue-order.md`, `attempt-records.md`, Taken, read 2026-10-10: base failure does not split/decrease, actual merge-to-fix increments the existing cap, priority preserves an active batch, and per-attempt outcome/time records are approved. 4. **Pitfalls and what removes each:** P1, off-by-one changes the meaning of 2 → currently `batch_limit` limits FOLLOWERS (`src/next.ts:1018-1020`). Define W as total and translate to at most W-1 followers. Total floor 1 requires zero followers. Keeping the existing follower floor at 1 would prevent solo convergence. P2, repeated events double-adjust W → apply update once per terminal attempt ID under the existing lock, with the persisted window and attempt reconciliation; count a reused green result as one landing, not another success. P3, a failed stack grows while being narrowed → freeze its membership, invalidate stale attempt IDs and shrink actual retry size, never refill from the queue. P4, turning BASE into false capacity pressure → take the locked red-main comparison/hold before split/decrease. P5, conflict penalties survive repair → preserve the approved clean-run solo clearing. P6, tiny green batches game the controller → grow once only when n equals W, not for every completion or every underfilled run. P7, a changed half inherits another half's green evidence → every actually landed top still passes the normal final gate and tested-top push guard, with existing record-only reuse as the sole approved exception. P8, declaring success from greener percentages → compare landed leaves per elapsed hour and per total attempt time, including repair stops and main regressions. Do not treat fewer attempted leaves as throughput improvement.

5. **Questions the fork must settle explicitly:** Q2, does “2” include the holder? Pick yes and record that contract everywhere. Q3, may success grow the window, explicitly reversing first-package's foreclosed growth option now that attempt records are approved? Pick yes, one step per full successful attempt. Q4, does a diagnostic retry use only failed membership or fresh queue candidates? Pick failed membership only. Current halving is reconstructed from the queue (`src/phase.ts:796`, `src/next.ts:1017-1020`), so a newly prioritized candidate can replace a previously tested member unless this is addressed. Q5, does the repo window survive process replacement? Pick yes, with exactly-once attempt adjustment and the already-required restart/reconciliation rules. An arbitrary configurable ceiling is not needed for this first change: growth only on full windows limits stored W to at most one more than the largest successfully filled window. This is not a resource-safety guarantee. Add a ceiling later only if measured gate cost or exposure requires one.

The smallest defensible change is outcome-controlled size with success recovery, total-size semantics, and narrowing that cannot admit new members. It does not require a probability estimator, a second queue, or speculative parallel merge turns.
