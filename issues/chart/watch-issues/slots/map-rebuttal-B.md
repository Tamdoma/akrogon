1. **F1 — An unchanged screen is neither necessary nor sufficient for a problem notification.** C4 adds a two-tick unchanged-screen condition that B did not recommend. A productive compile can remain visually unchanged, while an explicit fatal error warrants attention immediately. A stuck process can also keep printing. `src/next.ts:170-196` classifies lifecycle status and elapsed busy time, not progress. B's map, Q6/Pitfalls and R1, explicitly rejects unchanged-screen diagnosis. Replace the condition with concrete evidence from the bounded read and lifecycle/artifact context. Treat screen sameness only as supporting evidence, never as the deciding test or a required delay.

2. **F2 — The skill can avoid creating the cron, but cannot disclaim cancellation ownership while promising automatic stop.** Q3 says the skill “owns no cron,” whereas Q5 requires it to end the loop and Q6 stores the job ID. Fixed-interval loops continue until cancellation or expiry; CronDelete cancels by ID. Specify how the first tick identifies the exact job and records its ID, and have the skill delete that job on its stop condition. Do not guess when multiple matching jobs exist. Also keep the skill model-invocable: scheduled skills with `disable-model-invocation: true` are delivered as text rather than executed. Operator-only activation must be a prose/authorization rule compatible with scheduled invocation. Evidence: [Claude Code scheduled tasks, skill prompts, stopping and task management](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/scheduled-tasks). The updated `/loop` mechanism itself does not require a custom CronCreate wrapper.

3. **F3 — A Bash timeout around every tick command is not a safe lock-contention solution.** The map newly recommends timing out commands that may mutate lifecycle state. `src/state.ts:126-144` acquires the lock through a separate flock process; `src/phase.ts:113-131` commits state before notification and logging complete. Terminating a phase/next invocation can leave a committed action with an interrupted response or follow-up. It does not establish that no mutation happened or that retry is safe. Bound read-only observations if needed; do not use blanket cancellation of lifecycle mutations as the escape from contention. After any interrupted mutation, re-read authoritative state and report uncertainty before another action. This was not a shared B recommendation.

4. **F4 — `failure.cause = blocked` does not prove a human prerequisite.** C3 attributes that equivalence to B, but `src/phase.ts:198-208` assigns blocked to every explicit seat-declared failed stop, regardless of the reason. B recommended notify-only for actual human prerequisites and otherwise inspecting the reason and evidence. Replace the equivalence with “human prerequisite or unresolved blocker: notify, do not recover.” A blanket notify-only policy for all blocked-cause failures is a valid conservative option, but it must be presented as that policy choice, not a fact encoded by the cause enum.
