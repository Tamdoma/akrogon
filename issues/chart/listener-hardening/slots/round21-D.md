## Findings (both in the new tick rule's wording, skills/watch-issues/SKILL.md:17)

- F1 (low) Nothing tells the model that a compaction summary is not a conversation start. The stop fires on "a tick that is the first prompt of its conversation", judged by the model from its own context. A watcher runs up to 7 days of 20-minute checks with tool output, so auto-compaction is a matter of time; after it, the context opens with a continuation summary and the next tick is the first *visible* user prompt. A model that reads that as a fresh conversation deletes a healthy watch. One clause closes it ("a compaction summary before the tick means it is not the first prompt"). Same edge in the other direction, inherent to the discriminator and already visible in real history (`~/.gemini`... claude `history.jsonl` row 75: `/clear`, then `/effort`, then work): any first interaction after the clear — a settings command, a real prompt — spends the one stop chance, and later ticks check forever (clear-then-settings is a real sequence in the operator's own history, round 20's row 75, albeit in agy). That part cannot be worded away; it is the chosen heuristic's known miss, worth one sentence in the skill so the operator who clears-then-touches is not surprised.
- F2 (low) The tick rule pre-commits its reply against the stop steps it invokes. Tick bullet: "while CronList finds a job … it runs the stop steps below, replies 'Watch stopped: this conversation was cleared.' and runs no check." Stop bullet (:18): "several matches are reported and none are deleted." With two jobs, following both bullets is impossible — the stop steps deleted nothing, so "Watch stopped" is false, and since that tick used up the first-prompt condition, every later tick just checks: the cleared watch survives permanently in exactly the anomalous state the stop rule exists to report rather than hide. Make the reply follow the stop outcome ("replies with the stop's result") instead of scripting it.
