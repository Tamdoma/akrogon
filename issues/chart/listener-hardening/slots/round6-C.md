## What I could not prove

U1. Which process writes the reverts to t6x. Candidates, in my order: (a) moshi-hook's own window-follow (its attach is documented as "Show the Herdr window's (or sidebar's) selected terminal here", and it parses `herdr-app status` `focused_pane`), re-asserting the desktop focus on a timer or on its gateway polls; (b) the second phone session (the 11:37 mosh session is still alive and follows the same document) or the app re-asserting its last workspace; (c) the Moshi app itself after each chat-view refresh. Process sampling at 0.2 s for 210 s caught no `herdr-app status` or `hc connect` child of the daemon or the wrappers, and `ss -xp` does not show unix peers for this user, so the writer uses a connection it already holds. A `-v` daemon log or the moshi-hook source settles it; I did not restart the daemon.

U2. Whether the gateway's binding (mosh UDP port → `mosh-server` → shell → `herdr-core attach <t>`) fails during the child swap; the strings `no shell child for mosh-server on UDP %d` say it can.

## Root cause and the systemic difference from old herdr

Old herdr: the phone ran `herdr --session infra`, a TUI client with its own selection, switched from inside; one owner. hc has no TUI client, so moshi-hook built one out of three parts: a core-wide selection document, follower wrappers that re-exec `herdr-core attach` on every write, and a reconciliation with the desktop window's focus. Two owners now write one selection (the phone's choice and the window's focus, plus a second phone session), and every write tears down and rebuilds every viewer. That is the class; the "Connecting…" header, the per-tap redraws and the mixed workspace names are its symptoms.

## Smallest fix that removes the cause (owner: moshi-hook, rjyo/moshi app-hook)

The selection must have one owner per viewer: the attach follower keeps a per-session selection (or ignores selection writes it did not request), and the desktop window's focus is read only when the phone asks for it, never written back. Until moshi-hook changes, the operator has one lever that removes the follower entirely: the host's shell command becomes hc's own picker, `HC_SESSION=infra exec hc` (`hc` with no arguments is "pick a terminal and attach", `ctrl+\` detaches back to the picker; `hc --help`), the hc analogue of `herdr --session infra`: one owner, no shared document, no re-exec. Cost: tab changes from the app's chat view no longer move the terminal; the gateway sidebar still works (it binds by the `herdr-core attach <t>` child, P4). Low confidence on the picker's usability on a phone keyboard; test once. Closing the stale 11:37 session is a free cleanup either way.
