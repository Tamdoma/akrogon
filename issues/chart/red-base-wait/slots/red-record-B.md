## Q1. Entry identity, clearing and partial clearing

1. Pick, reason and cost: one active entry per actual failing test unit, with command/arguments/scope provenance and observations tied to the exact tested revision and material conditions. Identity includes runner/stage, file/suite and full case name where exposed. Do not use only a short test label or whole command. Keep multiple units from one red command separate, while preserving their shared comparison/log references. An entry is resolved by a fresh completed green result for that exact unit on the relevant target under equivalent conditions. A fix merge alone is a fix reference, not proof of resolution. One unit can resolve while another remains red. Keep the observed old-base evidence and resolution revision as history; do not claim the old revision became green or that a leaf's green result proves main green. Cost: resolution needs explicit evidence and context, not just a merge event. New observations update an existing incident without a notice per leaf or arbitrary SHA change. A demonstrated recurrence after resolution is a new incident and can notify again.
2. Rejected alternatives: one command-level entry cleared by any green part loses the other known failure and restores command-exit attribution. A notice keyed solely by SHA produces repeated noise on unrelated main changes. One global test-name entry across all conditions can exempt failures based on a different runtime or checkout layout. Clear on fix-leaf merge, issue closure or disappearance from output without green proof. Those events do not establish the test ran successfully. Delete all entries after main moves. That erases unresolved reds and makes an unrelated commit look like a repair. Retain a resolved entry as a blanket future exemption. That creates the stale-evidence acceptance bug.
3. Evidence: operator tier, waiting-leaf Taken requires per-test attribution, exact counterpart conditions and one notice per newly red check; comparison Taken forbids treating absent, skipped or unparseable units as pass. INTAKE.md #76 supplies C8 and motion-probe, and #77 supplies an environment repair outside git. merge-gate Taken explicitly keeps the stale-clear lesson active. better-than-training tier, src/phase.ts:857-864 and the old Hold schema record only one command/evidence path, so they cannot establish partial test resolution. src/status.ts:331-343 currently exposes health state independently of the leaf YAML, an existing place to show active units, evidence, observed revision and repair ownership without marking leaves failed.
4. Pitfalls and what removes them: lock then reread the exact incident/observation being resolved, and refuse a stale clear if a newer materially conflicting red observation arrived during proof. The lesson at learnings/history/2026-10-10-red-main-hold-stale-clear.md:6-8 requires guarding the decision, not only deletion. A green run of one case does not clear its suite. An interrupted suite may demonstrate an individual completed case but cannot prove required whole-command coverage or resolve unexecuted cases. Keep those claims separate. Resolve only the matching execution context and record what target became green; treat older observations as history rather than relabeling them. Native names can collide across runners, so preserve provenance. Persist incident identity and delivery outcome separately. Concurrent additions/joins must revalidate under the lock before deciding whether a notice is new. Retry an explicitly failed delivery only through an existing event/command with visible state; no timer or watchdog. A crash after showing a notice but before storing its acknowledgement prevents an exactly-once guarantee. State that delivery limit rather than silently dropping or claiming a notice.
5. Extra questions: does “newly red” mean a new failing unit, or a newly established distinct cause under the same unit? Recommend one notice per active unit/context incident, with observed cause/evidence updated and a new notice for confirmed recurrence. How are aliases or nested invocations that expose the same unit joined? Require supported equivalence judgment, not command-text normalization. What triggers the fresh resolution proof without a clock? Recommend existing repair verification or an explicit operator run, with accepted run evidence recorded. Base-evidence-reuse must settle the effect of a later green result or environment repair on old negative observations before handoff.

## Q2. Notice-only/operator ownership versus automatically filed seed

1. Pick, reason and cost: one incident notice to the operator, with a persistent repair reference when the operator assigns a single fix owner. The operator owns triage until that assignment; an issue seed alone is not an assigned owner. The notice/status names the unit, counterpart/conditions, evidence and whether repair ownership is pending or already linked. Unrelated leaves continue under Taken and do not independently repair recorded base defects outside their owned criteria. The designated fix leaf may address several unit entries from the same established cause. Existing linked intake/fix work is reused rather than reopened or duplicated. Cost: someone must choose and dispatch the repair. Recording an incident does not guarantee prompt repair, and this is a conscious tradeoff for avoiding automatic external filing and a second assignment workflow.
2. Rejected alternatives: automatically invoke seed-issue and claim that prevents duplicate fixes. It creates unverified intake, not a lifecycle contract, assignment or exclusive repair ownership. Independent seats can race through its lookups and both file or both fix. Silently select the first reporting leaf to repair every base defect expands its contract. Notify per leaf recreates today's noise. Keep the red only in a transient notice. That loses it after dismissal or a missed delivery. Automatically reopen a merged owner conflicts with existing failed-leaf routing. A unique repair reference is needed regardless of whether filing is later chosen.
3. Evidence: operator tier, INTAKE.md describes duplicate C8 fixes, and waiting-leaf/merge-gate Taken permits unrelated progress without forcing every discovery into repair. better-than-training tier, skills/seed-issue/SKILL.md:10,14-28 requires authenticated gh and destination routing, :67-84 performs searches and creates one report without lifecycle operations or assignment. Its failed lookup still permits posting (:67), so it is not concurrency-safe exclusive ownership. src/pull.ts:64-75 mirrors GitHub reports into issues/seeds; it does not turn them into fix leaves. skills/watch-issues/SKILL.md:38 tells the operator to chart a new fix leaf and never reopen a merged owner. src/phase.ts:87-109 records notification delivery, which distinguishes persistent incident knowledge from successful display.
4. Pitfalls and what removes them: a pending owner is visible and survives notice loss. Assign/link one repair contract before agents broaden work into that defect, and make reporting seats consult the existing reference instead of treating discovery as ownership. Keep the special case of a leaf-owned criterion separate: a required criterion still cannot be waived because base is red. Rewrite incidental base-test repair directions consistently with this ownership rule, including existing wrong-expectation exceptions in implement/check skills where they permit unrelated repair. Notification failure must not erase the incident or make the leaf fail for main health. If automatic seed filing is chosen instead, handoff must prove gh identity/routing and specify concurrency, ambiguous-create recovery and the seed URL record. No such filing/probe has been executed here. Even that choice still needs a separate owner assignment boundary.
5. Extra questions: is operator triage ownership sufficient until a fix contract exists, or must every first notice carry an already-known repair owner? The latter cannot be inferred from a test name or reporting leaf. Where is the unique repair reference assigned and checked, and does it point to a source issue, a fix leaf or both? These are material decisions for this fork, not a future feature. Decide whether an existing in-scope repair can claim a recorded incident through its contract without another operator round. Do not build a new automatic assignment system merely to file a seed.

## Q3. Green-on-retry results: shared red record or leaf evidence only?

1. Pick, reason and cost: keep inconsistent with-change results in the leaf's verification evidence, including both runs and the recorded retry budget. Do not put them in the shared known-red-on-base record without a completed without-change observation. A green retry alone establishes inconsistent outcomes for that candidate, not a broken base. The leaf's review can still identify and block an introduced flaky defect. Cost: there is no new repo-wide flaky-test inventory or automatic repair notice for every transient retry. Broader flake tracking would be a distinct outcome beyond the requested base-red record.
2. Rejected alternatives: treat every retry-green test as base-red. There is no counterpart proof, and shared reuse could exempt a genuine leaf regression. Mix confirmed base reds and inconsistent leaf runs under one status that consumers treat alike. That makes evidence strength ambiguous. Delete the first failed run when retry is green. That conceals potential new flakiness and resets the budget after a session replacement. Run a new base comparison merely to populate the record after a green retry. That contradicts comparison Taken's no-base-run disposition for that case.
3. Evidence: operator tier, comparison Taken explicitly records green-retry results on the leaf, performs no base run and retains review requirements. waiting-leaf Taken confines known-red to established counterpart failure. better-than-training tier, skills/check-issue/SKILL.md:49,59,61 ties review/criterion decisions to recorded evidence rather than a single successful status. The binding retry policy supplies the classification; no unsupported cause is inferred.
4. Pitfalls and what removes them: call the result inconsistent, not proven base flakiness. Keep the original failure visible to the review seat and preserve both run identities across resumed sessions. A confirmed changed-code flake is handled as a review defect despite retry success. Only a later valid counterpart comparison may establish a base incident, through the ordinary Q1 rule. Never use inconsistent leaf evidence as a substitute for a reusable negative counterpart result.
5. Extra question: should a repeated inconsistency that independently establishes a realistic defect be reported through the existing seed workflow? That can be an explicit report when warranted, but is not automatic inclusion in this red record. If the operator wants a shared inventory of all flakes, chart that as a separate destination outcome instead of silently widening this record.
