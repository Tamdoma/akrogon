## Q1. Stocks and flows

- S3 — Integration drift. New main commits add divergence while a branch is being implemented, reviewed or waiting. Refresh/rebase consumes divergence but can expose incompatible assumptions, as P/review-B.md:477 shows. Implementation refreshes and records before/after SHAs (A/skills/implement-issue/SKILL.md:40), and merge refreshes again (A/docs/guide/merge.md:7). Verification itself creates a fresh checkout and runs setup plus commands (A/src/verify.ts:124–171). More queued work need not mean more concurrent CPU, but overlapping runs can compete. The load-to-timeout connection here is operator evidence in brief.md, not independently measured in this pass.

## Q4. Avoid

- N1 — Restoring full suites on every leaf to improve the displayed merge percentage. That moves detection and cost earlier, may amplify contention, and still needs the integrated gate. Current separation is explicit in F/issues/config.yaml:8–12 and A/src/verify.ts:49–56.
- N2 — Raising retries/timeouts, adding concurrency caps, or adding a dependency-selection engine as the first response. These do not remove duplicated work or repair the observed producer/consumer mismatch. A timeout increase is justified only if the bounded operation itself needs it, not as a substitute for diagnosing contention. These are design judgments, not a claim that such tools never help.
- N3 — Weakening expectations merely to turn red green, skipping the full gate, or treating every repeat failure as a leaf defect. The exact-list assertion can change only while preserving its useful coverage guarantee. Product behavior and real recorded outputs remain the acceptance target (A/skills/check-issue/SKILL.md:53).
