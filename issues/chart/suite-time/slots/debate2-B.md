# Slot B: O6 implementation judgment and updated ranking

Measured 2026-10-25. Read-only inspection and ledger calculations only in this round. Original O1–O5 references remain unchanged.

## O6: conditional option, not the proposed small generic patch

D1. KEEP O6 for a policy/design decision, but DROP “break on first sequential red” as ready to implement. It preserves the requirement for eventual green evidence only if blocked work has a recovery route. The current known-red route does not provide that route.

F1. Recomputed from issues/verify-runs.jsonl: 251 completed setup-green runs, 90 red, 48 format-red and 35 format-only red. Joining command.log to issues/timings.jsonl check_end.log gives 7,220.120 test seconds inside format-red runs. Total completed verify duration is 43,640.885 s; red verify duration is 18,213.211 s.
F2. Therefore the gross removable test execution is 16.54% of all completed verify duration or 39.64% of red verify duration. O6 clears 20% on the latter baseline, not the former. These are summed execution durations, not CPU measurements or net workflow savings. With all post-format commands included, the observed suffix costs 7,325.944 s.
F3. C's “no usable evidence” claim is wrong. Thirteen format-red runs also had another red command. Their output can identify a second defect before the next repair cycle. A formatting fix usually changes the tree and invalidates receipt reuse, but it does not erase diagnostic value. Formatting errors need not always require a tree change in other repos, either.

R1. Receipt gates: blocked must remain non-green. Current schema accepts blocked; greenEntry requires exit=0; requireGreenReceipt requires evidence for every expected command. Copying a nonzero blocker exit safely refuses admission, but does not make the run complete proof. Preserve command names/text, blocked attribution and scope. Never relax gates to count “not run” as covered.
R2. Known-red: this is a concrete compatibility defect in the proposal, not merely a test gap. Consider checks {first: a named test failing identically on head/base, after: a passing check}. Fail-fast records after as blocked on both trees. knownRedReceipt explicitly skips blocked entries, so it cannot establish after green or covered. verify --known-red reuses the recorded red head and runs only a base comparison, leaving after blocked again. Repeating the command cannot recover fresh head evidence.
R2 removal: either constrain fail-fast to a separately justified terminal precondition or design execution of the blocked suffix after upstream known-red coverage. Do not treat a blocked command as a known-red failure. This needs explicit design and a two-command known-red regression case before implementation.
R3. After a red merge, src/land.ts hands off the completed red receipt ID. check.fix reproduces the actual failing command, while blocked commands have no independent failing output. Ensure the handoff names the first executed failure, never prescribes reproducing the blocked suite, and performs a fresh complete verification after repair. A second defect may require another merge/repair turn; that is an unmeasured cost.
R4. Other repos: declared order may put an expensive check first, producing no saving. An early known-red test may suppress independent useful checks. Respecting order prevents reordering bugs but does not prevent lost diagnostics or extra repair cycles. Do not claim “no repo gets worse”. Audit affected configured sequences and known-red use before choosing a generic behavior.
R5. Existing behavior is deliberate: tests/verify.test.ts “verify runs every leaf check...” asserts after-red executes; its same-tree rerun test reuses the green after-red command. The prior operator decision recorded in issues/chart/verify-waste/forks/receipts.md “Refined at draft review” retains merge-member execution after red. That decision directly concerns members, so its application to sequential checks needs a scope decision. Either way, O6 changes the tested sequential behavior and completeness of diagnostics. It is not already authorized by the existing blocked-members pattern.

A1. Size: a naive implementation is about 20–30 source lines plus 40–80 test lines in src/verify.ts and tests/verify.test.ts. Known-red compatibility and the changed diagnostic contract make that estimate incomplete. A defensible leaf likely also touches the repo check order and affected lifecycle wording. Do not lock a 15-line implementation before R2 is resolved. Reordering typecheck ahead of test is separate from generic fail-fast and saves nothing by itself without it.
A2. Proof: first show format-red blocks expensive execution while gates refuse incomplete evidence, then prove recovery after repair, mixed green reuse, covered first-command red with a fresh suffix, and merge handoff behavior. Compare paired workloads containing both cheap-only and multiple failures. “100 future runs” alone does not control tree size, red mix or added repair/queue rounds. Measure total verification and repair cost, not only time until the first error.

## C4, C5 and E12

D2. C4 is a valid cost-pool observation, not a fix. issues/log.jsonl filtered since 2026-10-12 has 57 review→repair and 43 review→merge exits; repair exits are 37→check.fix and 20→merge. There are 63 merged events, 25 with fix_rounds>=1. These do not show that review was wasteful. Reviews may prevent defects, repeated visits are not independent leaves, and the transitions alone do not prove an extra verify per visit. Keep investigation only: classify actual Fix causes before proposing one small change or a >=20% benefit.

D3. C5's grouping is directionally useful, but its arithmetic is wrong. Calling src/flaky.ts flips(repo, readVerifyRuns(repo)) directly yields 85 flip events over THIRTEEN end times, including one missing-log placeholder. The two late recovery end times contain 42+11=53 events, not 42. flakyLines shows 57 summary rows with EIGHT distinct last end times because it aggregates repeated names and retains only each name's latest time. Those summary rows are not the raw events.
F4. C5's ~0.7 h estimate also undercounts repeated reds. Group completed setup-green test receipts by tree/scope/command and scan by end: there are 13 red→green transitions, but 20 preceding red test executions, totaling 6,441.760 s (1.79 h) by the timing join. This does not prove all those executions could be removed, but multiplying transition count by a generic 170 s is not their measured cost.
R6. The observed start-time concurrency excludes Bun's within-file parallelism and unrelated host activity. It cannot rule out resource pressure. Conversely, clustered recovery does not make every named failure a load defect. O4's inspected timestamp bug remains a specific demonstrated cause. Keep C5 as an investigation guide, not a blanket timeout or concurrency fix.

D4. E12 is real: 25 of 276 receipts have no end and empty commands. In this read, all 25 recorded worktree paths and /proc/<recorded pid> paths are absent, so the records do not demonstrate 25 currently running leaks. runOnce appends the empty initial receipt before checkout/setup and updates commands/end later, so an empty receipt does not prove no work occurred. Keep an interrupted-run observation, not an optimization with invented savings. Correlate receipt PID/run path, command timings, run-unit records and stop history before attributing cause or duration. Never manufacture completion or green evidence.

## Updated final ranking

D5. Ready improvement order remains: (1) O1a shorten unnecessary fixture budgets, (2) O1b separate nine serial named-target cases, (3) O4 anchor status-fixture timestamps, (4) O1c only if a small test seam preserves timeout-path coverage, (5) O2 only if its narrow frequency warrants the scope-sharing change. Their baselines and dependencies remain those in debate-B.md. O1's combined >=20% suite saving remains an acceptance target, not a proven result.
D6. Conditional decisions: consider O6 ahead of O3 once its diagnostic-policy decision and R2 recovery design are settled. It retains eventual check coverage but trades complete first-pass diagnostics for earlier feedback. O3 remains the larger raw compute option only if the operator accepts deferring full-suite feedback to merge. Neither enters the automatic implementation queue from this debate alone.
D7. Drop O5 blanket timeout increases. C4/C5/E12 remain evidence-backed investigations without a demonstrated small fix or >=20% gain. No new implementation leaf follows merely from their counts.
