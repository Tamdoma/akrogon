Disputes only.

- R1 Item 5, my rejection of `--max-concurrency` is overstated as merged, and I withdraw the blanket form. My evidence was one file run alone (batch-dispatch 5.74 s at 20, 10.78 s at 4). Run alone, that file's own tests are the only work that can fill 32 cores. In the full check at `--parallel=4`, four files feed the cores at once, so a per-file cap of 4 does not starve the host the same way. My single-file ratio does not transfer to the suite. A cap is a legitimate candidate to trial in the S1 proof, as B proposes.
- R2 Item 5, A's whole-suite numbers do not settle the cap either way. One run each, load rising across the sequence (3 to 15, then 15 to 48, then 48 to 62), and a framework verify on the host during them. The middle setting (8) came out slowest and took the only failures, which no cap theory predicts. Noise and outside load dominate these three runs. What they do show: at suite level the cap-4 penalty is nothing like my single-file 1.9x, since cap 20 at load 48 to 62 (116.4 s) and cap 4 at load 3 to 15 (124.5 s) land close, and both are far under the 192 to 216 s serial baseline. Settling it needs interleaved paired runs (20, 4, 20, 4) at recorded load, not three sequential singles.
