## Q1. Pre-merge base red

1. **Pick D1: (a), with merge as the single authority for accepting an unrelated red check.** Before merge, seats still run their checks, finish the leaf's own criteria and repair failures attributable to their work. An unrelated general-check failure is recorded honestly and proceeds to the next normal phase, with its final disposition deferred to merge. It is not reported as passing. No pre-merge wait or new recovery state is needed. Under S1, merge freshly checks the integrated candidate and its exact base, then waits only that holder if the candidate remains red. Cost: work can accumulate at merge until main changes or the per-test merge rule lands, and a newly introduced failure missed during local attribution is found later at merge.

   I would **make the red-base-wait chain smaller**: the standalone pre-merge policy in known-red-leaf becomes unnecessary. Fold its comparison schema, validation, red record and notices into known-red-merge, which remains necessary. Preserve the per-test merge acceptance rule and fresh base-run evidence. This is a proposal for this fork, requiring an operator answer and contract amendments. Round 1 alone did not authorize cancelling known-red-leaf or removing those record features.

2. **Rejected options.** (b) preserves the same dependency on main earlier in the workflow, adds another wait/wake path and can stop a main-repairing change before it reaches the final gate. The existing comparison chain would remain necessary and unchanged apart from replacing the stop with a wait. (c) keeps the pre-merge stop for the recorded base-red class while the dependency chain lands. Its comparison chain stays unchanged. Also reject treating every nonzero check as unrelated, passing an unproved own criterion, or simply deleting known-red-leaf while known-red-merge still consumes its exports.

3. **Evidence.** Operator tier: `night/fork-failed-exits-brief-B.md:8` requests moving work with less complexity, and `night/fork-stop-rules.md:8-14` already assigns waiting to merge. Better-than-training: `skills/implement-issue/SKILL.md:38` and `skills/check-issue/SKILL.md:65` create the pre-merge failed exits. `issues/open/red-base-wait/known-red-leaf/brief.md:18` owns both pre-merge policy and the shared record machinery. `issues/open/red-base-wait/known-red-merge/brief.md:6-8,24` consumes that machinery and binds acceptance to the tested stack and `batch.built_on`. Remove this through the final per-test comparison, complete logs, causal review and own-criteria refusals already required by known-red-merge. Until then S1 permits no red push. ## Q2. Unreachable seats and undelivered prompts

1. **Pick D2: keep the product phase and recover the delivery, using the existing dispatcher.** Remove the automatic failed moves for these transport conditions. Keep the current error, attempt count and delivery evidence visible. Give each actual new launch a fresh valid agent name instead of a name derived solely from the public pane ID. On `agent_not_running`, reread the owned pane and session. Relaunch only when its agent has really exited and its shell is available. If the process moved or the prompt arrived late, reconcile its identity or delivery instead. Preserve worktree contents, artifacts, phase, completed votes and fix rounds. Retry undelivered prompts at most once per dispatch pass. A third pass is diagnostic evidence, not proof that the product needs a human decision. Cost: delivery faults can remain pending while the runtime is unavailable, and the mechanism needs a reliable next-pass trigger.

2. **Rejected options.** Keep automatic failed: it turns a runtime fault into operator-only phase recovery. Merely increase the count: it delays the same stop. Blindly resend or restart at the threshold: a timeout can follow successful delivery, and `agent_not_running` can follow a pane move rather than a dead process. Reuse the same conflicting name: the next launch repeats the same error. A new recovery phase, daemon or global restart is unnecessary for these local failures. Credential prompts and actual human permissions remain human blockers and are not bypassed.

4. **Lifetime pitfalls.** R4: duplicate prompts can launch duplicate work. Keep delivery reconciliation, bind the stored offset to its recorded session, and revalidate the phase before sending.
